## Frame 3 — Read the answer, then open the source

- src: compositions/frames/scene-3.html

- transition_in: cut
- scene: check-ai / demonstration
- photo: none: full-stage authored demonstration
- worked states: Answer|A lead, not yet a source; Source|Publisher · update date · fit; Decide|Pages disagree: confirm by phone
- voiceover: "An AI answer is a quick summary written from many pages. It can sound sure and still be wrong, out of date, or about a different town. So treat it as a lead, not a source. Look for the links beside or beneath it, and open the organization's own page. Ask who published it, when it was updated, and whether it answers your question. If the summary and the page disagree, trust the responsible organization, and confirm hours by phone before you travel. Never type private details into a question, such as a Social Security number, a bank number, or medical information. Pause, open one source from your answer, and find one detail it confirms and one it does not."

- voiceover: "Suppose you find a useful service today and need it again next month. A note saying good website will not be enough. Record the organization, the page title, the web address, the date you checked it, and the particular fact you plan to use. In your handout, distinguish a checked fact from your own interpretation. If the page does not answer a question, write needs confirmation rather than filling the gap with a guess. This is the digital equivalent of writing the source beside a number in a household notebook."

- voiceover: "Sync is convenient because a change can travel between a device and an online service. The same convenience can carry a deletion.
