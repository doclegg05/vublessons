# Week 2 structure and content review (Tell, Show, Do, Review + WIPPEA + adult learning)

## Verdict
The lesson contains excellent, highly relevant content and brilliant low-risk simulations that are perfect for older adults, but its structure is fundamentally flawed. By grouping the instruction into two massive 20-minute presentation blocks followed by long labs, it violates the short-cycle Tell-Show-Do-Review principle, which will overwhelm the learners' working memory. The single most important change is to break the two-hour session into five short, distinct cycles (e.g., Search, Forms, Folders, Sharing, Recovery) where learners practice and are checked immediately after each specific skill is shown.

| **Evaluation** | Lesson plan 110–120 min, Slides 19–20 | Weak | All checks are left to the very end of the two-hour class. | Move Knowledge Checks and worksheet reviews to the end of each short cycle. |
| **Application** | Slides 21–23, Video 6:30 | Adequate | Connects the skills to a repeatable routine for home use. | Keep as the final wrap-up. |

**Overall flow:** The current flow follows a linear, outdated lecture-then-lab model rather than the cyclical WIPPEA model. Each objective does not get its own presentation, practice, and check; instead, they are bundled together, meaning a learner who gets lost at minute 15 will remain lost for the next hour. The video is treated as a supplementary explainer block rather than being embedded chapter-by-chapter as the "Show" step for each specific task.

The week completely fails to follow Tell, Show, Do, Review as short cycles, opting instead for big blocks (20 minutes of Tell/Show, then 20–30 minutes of Do). For older adult learners, this high cognitive load means they will likely forget the steps for the first skill by the time they are allowed to touch their keyboards.

## Objectives
1. **Refine a search and justify a source choice.**
   - *ABCD rewrite:* Given a local community need (Condition), the learner (Audience) will refine a web search and justify a source choice (Behavior) by recording evidence-based judgments on the worksheet (Degree).
   - *Status:* Taught (Slides 3–8), Practiced (Worksheet 1–4), Evaluated (Slide 19, Worksheet check).
2. **Complete a fictional online form and check its confirmation.**
   - *ABCD rewrite:* Given a simulated online form (Condition), the learner (Audience) will complete the form and verify the confirmation message (Behavior) without entering real personal data (Degree).
   - *Status:* Taught (Slides 9–10), Practiced (Worksheet 5), Evaluated (Worksheet check).
3. **Organize files, set access and explain a recovery path.**
   - *ABCD rewrite:* Given a fictional file (Condition), the learner (Audience) will organize the file, set sharing permissions, and identify a recovery path (Behavior) accurately according to the worksheet guide (Degree).
   - *Status:* Taught (Slides 11–18), Practiced (Worksheet 6–8), Evaluated (Slide 20, Worksheet check).

  - **What is correct:** Pick one consistent filename and extension for the entire lesson so learners can track the file's journey.

  - **Severity:** Low
- **Where:** Worksheet 8
  - **What it says:** "Explain when to use version history... Explain why ZIP..."
  - **What is correct:** While technically correct, asking for two long written explanations in one task is a poor assessment method for this audience.
  - **Severity:** Low

## Does the content make sense for this audience?
- **Pacing:** The pacing in the lesson plan is unrealistic. Expecting older adults to sit through 20 minutes of software demonstrations and then remember the steps for a 20-minute lab is a leap in logic regarding working memory.
- **Dexterity:** Worksheet Task 8 asks learners to "Explain when to use version history... Explain why ZIP and read-only do not prove encryption." For older veterans who may have reduced dexterity, typing or writing out long paragraphs is frustrating and unnecessary. This should be a verbal check or a practical demonstration.
- **Safety:** The use of "Fictional local simulations" (Slides 5, 10, 13, 15, 18) is brilliant for this audience. It removes the fear of "breaking the computer" or accidentally sharing private information, which is a major barrier for older learners.

## Recommended restructure
*(120 minutes total. Incorporating the required 25-minute pre-test and breaking into short cycles.)*
- **0–25 (25 min): Pre-test** (Required evaluation).
- **25–35 (10 min): Warm-up & Intro.** Review Week 1. Introduce Week 2 scenario (Slides 1–2, Video 0:00).
- **35–50 (15 min): Cycle 1 - Search & Evaluate.** Tell/Show: Slides 3–7, Video 0:44 & 1:24. Do: Worksheet 1–4. Review: Slide 19.
- **50–60 (10 min): Cycle 2 - Source Trail & Forms.** Tell/Show: Slides 8–10, Video 2:09 & 2:47. Do: Worksheet 5. Review: Instructor checks screens.
- **60–70 (10 min): Break.** Pause away from screens.
- **70–85 (15 min): Cycle 3 - Folders & Sync.** Tell/Show: Slides 11, 13, Video 3:30 & 5:06. Do: Worksheet 6. Review: Instructor checks folders.
- **85–100 (15 min): Cycle 4 - Sharing Access.** Tell/Show: Slides 12, 15, Video 4:13. Do: Worksheet 7. Review: Instructor checks simulator.
- **100–115 (15 min): Cycle 5 - Protection & Recovery.** Tell/Show: Slides 16–18, Video 5:49. Do: Worksheet 8 (verbal/demo). Review: Slide 20.
- **115–120 (5 min): Application.** Wrap-up and real-life connection (Slides 21–23, Video 6:30).
