# Week 2 video evaluation

## Verdict
This video requires significant instructor support to be usable in class. While the visual design is highly accessible and the concepts are accurate, the pacing is too dense for older novices, the interfaces are overly abstracted, and the practice prompts fail to provide actual time to pause. Overall score: 2.5 out of 5.

## Evidence by dimension

### 1. Accuracy and correctness
- **3:45**: The explanation that a file saved locally may not appear on another device, while a cloud file belongs to an account, is accurate and foundational.
- **4:20**: The definitions of Viewer, Commenter, and Editor roles accurately reflect standard cloud sharing permissions (like Google Drive or OneDrive).
- **5:20**: The demonstration that deleting a synced file removes it from all locations is a crucial and correct warning.

### 2. Clarity of explanation
- **1:24**: The distinction between a "Directory lead" and a "Responsible source" is a great concept, but explaining it using only abstract text boxes rather than showing a real web page makes it harder to grasp.
- **4:47**: The narrator suddenly says, "distinguish read-only from encryption." Encryption has not been defined, introducing unnecessary jargon.
- **6:10**: The explanation of a ZIP file correctly distinguishes it from a backup, but the visual is just three abstract boxes, which doesn't clarify *how* to use one.

### 3. Pacing and cognitive load for older adults
- **2:09**: The "source record" chapter introduces five different pieces of metadata to record, which is a lot to absorb in 38 seconds.
- **5:06 to 6:30**: In just 84 seconds, the video covers sync vs. backup, version history, the recycle bin, and ZIP compression. This is a massive cognitive overload for learners who are not confident with technology.
- **6:30**: The final summary asks learners to recall and apply almost every concept in the video at once.

### 4. Practice prompts
- **1:13**: "Pause and write a search..." The narration continues immediately with no visual cue to pause.
- **4:57**: "Pause and choose a role..." The narrator gives away the answer ("Viewer is the appropriate starting point") just two seconds later.
- **5:20**: "Watch the practice file disappear..." This is an observation prompt, but it happens very quickly.

### 5. Audio–visual alignment
- **3:35**: As the narrator says "Create a practice folder," the cursor moves to and clicks the "+ New folder" button.
- **4:28**: The cursor clicks "Viewer", "Commenter", and "Editor" exactly as the narrator defines each role.
- **5:20**: Trash can icons appear over the synced files at the exact moment the narrator mentions a deletion spreading.

### 6. Screen demonstrations
- **3:30**: The cursor is large, and clicks are clearly indicated with a yellow expanding circle, which is excellent for novices.
- **4:13**: The sharing interface is clean and easy to read.
- **General**: Because all interfaces are highly simplified simulations (e.g., "Practice Files" instead of Windows Explorer), learners may struggle to map these clean screens to the cluttered reality of their actual computers.

### 7. Visual design and legibility
- **0:44**: The dark blue background with white and yellow text provides excellent contrast for older eyes or a washed-out classroom projector.
- **2:47**: The text size in the "Practice request" form is large and easily legible at 720p.
- **5:06**: The diagrams are completely free of unnecessary clutter or decorative elements (Mayer's Coherence Principle).

### 8. Narration quality
- **0:00**: The pronunciation is crisp and the pacing of individual sentences is deliberate.
- **2:09**: The voice lacks natural breath sounds and maintains a very rigid pitch, suggesting it may be synthetic/AI-generated, which reduces warmth.
- **6:30**: The tone remains encouraging ("Choose a useful task to repeat independently").

### 9. Relevance and motivation
- **0:00**: Framing the entire lesson around helping a neighbor find community resources (like a veteran's organization) is highly motivating and respects their adult experience.
- **2:47**: Practicing on a fictional form builds confidence without the fear of accidentally sending real data or breaking something.

### 10. Accessibility
- **3:35**: The narrator says "Create a practice folder" but does not read the on-screen button text ("Plus New folder"). A visually impaired learner wouldn't know what to look for.
- **5:15**: The narrator says "Watch the practice file disappear" but does not describe the visual (trash cans appearing over the documents).
- **General**: No information is conveyed by color alone; text labels accompany all icons.

## Top issues, ranked

1. **High**: Practice prompts do not provide time to pause and sometimes spoil the answer (1:13, 4:57). This defeats the purpose of active learning for this audience. **Fix**: instructor note (no edit) — the instructor must keep their hand on the pause button.
2. **High**: Extreme information density in chapters 8 and 9 (5:06–6:30). Five major file management concepts are rushed through in under 90 seconds. **Fix**: instructor note (no edit) — pause after each concept and demonstrate it live.
3. **Medium**: Over-reliance on abstract diagrams instead of software interfaces (1:24, 5:49). Novices will struggle to map boxes and arrows to real web browsers and file explorers. **Fix**: instructor note (no edit) — show real-world equivalents on the projector.
4. **Low**: Unexplained jargon (4:47). The sudden mention of "encryption" will confuse learners. **Fix**: instructor note (no edit) — tell the class to ignore that specific term.

## Strengths to keep
- **0:00**: The framing scenario of helping a neighbor is excellent and respectful of the audience.
- **0:44**: The visual contrast and text size are perfect for older adults.
- **3:30**: The large cursor and yellow click-indicators make following the pointer very easy.
- **4:13**: The advice to choose the smallest access role (least privilege) is excellent digital hygiene.

## Tips for the instructor using this video on Monday
- Keep your hand hovering over the pause button. The video will tell learners to "Pause and..." but it will not actually stop playing.
- At 4:57, hit pause *immediately* when you hear "Pause and choose a role," otherwise the narrator gives away the answer two seconds later.
- The interfaces shown in the video are simplified simulations. After chapter 6 (3:30), take a moment to show the class what creating a folder looks like in actual Windows File Explorer.
- Chapters 8 and 9 (5:06) move incredibly fast. Play them one at a time, pause, and explain the difference between sync and backup using a physical analogy (like a mirror vs. a photocopier).
- Tell the class not to worry about the word "encryption" mentioned briefly at 4:47; it is not needed for this week's tasks.
- The final prompt at 6:30 asks them to do five things at once. Break this down on the whiteboard and have them do it one step at a time.
