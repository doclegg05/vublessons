# Week 4 structure and content review (Tell, Show, Do, Review + WIPPEA + adult learning)

## Verdict
The lesson contains highly accurate, vendor-neutral content that is perfectly tailored to the real-life needs of older adults, but its structural pacing is flawed. It groups instruction into two massive "Tell/Show" blocks followed by long "Do" labs, which will overwhelm the working memory of this audience. The single most important change is to break the 20-minute and 30-minute lab blocks into five short Tell-Show-Do-Review cycles, allowing learners to practice and verify each specific skill immediately after seeing it demonstrated.

**Overall flow:** The current order violates the cyclical nature of WIPPEA and Tell, Show, Do, Review. By grouping the lesson into a "Communication model" block and a "Collaboration and commerce" block, the instructor presents up to six distinct concepts before the learner touches the mouse. Furthermore, Slide 15 awkwardly places the entire chaptered video in the middle of the lesson, contradicting the lesson plan's instruction to use chapters during demonstrations. The video chapters must be integrated into the "Show" phase of individual skill cycles.

**Cycle analysis:** The week currently follows big blocks rather than short cycles. For older adult learners, this means they will likely forget the nuances of "Bcc" or "Scheduled send" by the time they are asked to draft their email 20 minutes later. Restructuring into micro-cycles will drastically improve confidence and retention.

## Content correctness
- **Medium Severity:** Slide 15 ("Watch: collaborate without confusion") implies the learner should sit and watch the entire video at once. However, the lesson plan correctly states: "Use relevant chapters during the demonstration phases... Replay only the chapter needed." *Correction:* Remove Slide 15 and embed/play the specific video chapters during the relevant "Show" phases.
- **Low Severity (Confusing):** Worksheet Task 3 asks learners to "Explain To, Cc and Bcc. Choose an appropriate time and account for your message." This combines two completely different concepts (email routing vs. digital identity/timing) into a single task. *Correction:* Split this into two separate worksheet tasks.
- **No errors found:** The technical explanations of Bcc (Slide 5), version history (Slide 13), meeting controls (Slide 14), and in-app purchases (Slide 18) are highly accurate for 2026 environments (Windows 11, M365, Google Workspace) and avoid common misleading simplifications.

## Does the content make sense for this audience?
Yes, the tone is respectful and the use of fictional data ("example.invalid", "Practice Learning Store") creates a safe, low-risk environment for older adults who may fear making a real mistake or sending an accidental email. However, there is a **missing logistical step**: the lesson plan tells the instructor to "Provide... a word processor and spreadsheet app" and "Use instructor-provided fictional accounts," but does not explain *how* learners will access the shared document for Worksheet Tasks 5 and 6. In a typical lab, if the instructor hasn't pre-emailed a live collaboration link, learners cannot practice synchronous coauthoring. The instructor must be prompted to set up and distribute this link beforehand.

## Recommended restructure
*This revised 120-minute running order incorporates the required 25-minute pre-test and breaks the lesson into short Tell-Show-Do-Review cycles.*

- **00–25 (25 min): Pre-test** (Required course element).
- **25–35 (10 min): Warm-up & Intro.** Reconnect (Lesson plan), Slide 1, Video 0:00.
- **35–50 (15 min): Cycle 1 — Channels & Identity.** 
  - *Tell:* Slides 2, 3. 
  - *Show:* Video 0:48, 1:06. 
  - *Do/Review:* Worksheet 1 & 3 (Identity part).
- **50–65 (15 min): Cycle 2 — Email Drafting.** 
  - *Tell:* Slides 4, 5, 6, 7. 
  - *Show:* Video 1:36, 2:18. Instructor demos inclusive language. 
  - *Do/Review:* Worksheet 2, 3 (To/Cc/Bcc part), 4.

- **75–90 (15 min): Cycle 3 — Coauthoring & Feedback.**

## Top 10 changes, ranked
1. **Restructure into short TSDR cycles (Large effort):** Break the 20-minute presentation and 30-minute lab blocks into five micro-cycles (as outlined above) to prevent cognitive overload and honor adult learning pacing.
2. **Add the 25-minute pre-test (Small effort):** Insert the pre-test at the very beginning of the lesson plan to align with course requirements.
3. **Delete Slide 15 (Small effort):** Remove the standalone video slide and update the lesson plan to explicitly trigger video chapters during the "Show" phase of each specific cycle.
4. **Move Community Slides 8–9 (Small effort):** Move these slides to sit immediately after Slide 14 (Meetings) so that all "Participating Online" content is grouped together, preventing an interruption in the email drafting flow.
5. **Split Worksheet Task 3 (Small effort):** Separate the task into "3a: Explain To, Cc, Bcc" and "3b: Choose an appropriate time and account" so they can be practiced in their respective cycles.
6. **Clarify shared document logistics (Medium effort):** Add a note to the "Prepare the room" section of the lesson plan detailing exactly how the instructor will distribute the shared document link for the coauthoring lab (Tasks 5 & 6).
7. **Add a "Show" demo for Inclusive Language (Medium effort):** Since there is no video chapter for Slide 7, add an instructor note to physically demonstrate revising a sentence on the projector before learners attempt Worksheet Task 4.
8. **Update the Warm-up (Small effort):** Change the reconnect prompt to "Share one improvement from the resource-pack review, and discuss how you would share that pack with a group," bridging Week 3 to Week 4.
9. **Add an Email Knowledge Check (Medium effort):** Create a new slide after Slide 7 with a multiple-choice question about Bcc or Reply All to evaluate the email cycle before moving on.
10. **Prompt active instructor review (Small effort):** Update the lesson plan to explicitly instruct the facilitator to walk the room and check screens during each short "Do" phase, rather than waiting for the end of the lesson to collect evidence.
