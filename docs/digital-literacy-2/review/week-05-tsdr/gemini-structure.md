# Week 5 structure and content review (Tell, Show, Do, Review + WIPPEA + adult learning)

## Verdict
The lesson contains highly relevant, practical safety content for older adults, but the current structure violates the Tell, Show, Do, Review framework by clumping all instruction into a massive 20-minute block, followed by a written worksheet. The single most important change is to break the "Safety model" and "Scenario practice" into short, alternating micro-cycles (e.g., teach phishing, practice phishing; teach permissions, practice permissions) and to change the worksheet from written definitions to hands-on computer tasks.

**Overall flow:** The macro-order (Warm-up, New Content, Break, Evaluation) is logical for a test day. However, the micro-flow of the new content is flawed. The lesson plan groups all "Tell/Show" into minutes 10–30, and all "Do" into minutes 30–45. For older learners, holding six different technical and behavioral concepts in working memory for 20 minutes before practicing is a recipe for cognitive overload. The video is also treated as a single asset rather than being paused after each chapter for immediate practice.

**Cycle Analysis:** The week follows massive blocks, not short cycles. For older veterans, this requires unlearning the military "firehose" style of instruction. They need immediate, tactile application of a concept to anchor it before moving to the next. 

## Objectives

1. **Explain device, file and account protections.**
   * *ABCD Rewrite:* Given a fictional scenario (Condition), the learner (Audience) will identify the correct device, file, or account protection (Behavior) with 100% accuracy (Degree).
   * *Alignment:* Taught (Slides 10-13), Practiced weakly (WS Q5-6), Evaluated (Post-test).
2. **Recognize manipulation and choose a safe next step.**
   * *ABCD Rewrite:* Given a simulated urgent message or interaction (Condition), the learner (Audience) will state the unverified claims and choose an independent verification route (Behavior) without clicking suspicious links (Degree).
   * *Alignment:* Taught (Slides 6-9), Practiced (WS Q3-4), Evaluated (Post-test).
3. **Demonstrate core tasks and complete the parallel post-test.**
   * *ABCD Rewrite:* Given a computer workstation and previous lab files (Condition), the learner (Audience) will perform seven core Level 2 tasks and complete the post-test (Behavior), achieving a baseline score to guide future practice (Degree).
   * *Alignment:* Taught (Past weeks), Practiced/Evaluated (WS Q7, Post-test).

## Content correctness

| Where | What it says | What is correct | Severity |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| **Worksheet Q7; Answer Guide Q7** | "Open supplies.csv... Add a Total cell... A formula such as =SUM(B2:B4)" | CSV (Comma Separated Values) files are plain text. They **do not save formulas**. If a learner types a formula, saves, and closes, the formula is lost. | **High.** This will cause learners to fail the task or think they broke the computer. Change the file to `supplies.xlsx`, or change the task to "Calculate the total and type the resulting number." |
| **Slide 10; Video 2:50** | Implies a password to open a file is separate from encryption. | In modern Microsoft 365 (Word/Excel) and Adobe Acrobat, applying a "Password to Open" *is* the mechanism that encrypts the file (AES-256). | **Medium.** Clarify that a password to open is often the "key" that turns on the encryption. |
| **Slide 4** | "Keyboard route: 1 Tab: Search, 2 Tab: Filter, 3 Enter: select action" | Tab order is entirely dependent on how a specific website is coded. It is not universally 2 tabs to reach a filter. | **Low.** Add the word "Example:" so novices don't think pressing Tab twice always opens a filter. |
| **Video 2:09** | "Use Contact on the known website" | In 2026, many sites use AI chatbots or Help Centers rather than a simple "Contact Us" page. | **Low.** Mention that they might need to look for "Help" or "Support" links at the bottom of the page. |

## Does the content make sense for this audience?
Yes, with one major caveat: **Pacing and Modality.** 
Older adults, especially those with potential vision, hearing, or dexterity challenges, cannot sit through a 20-minute lecture and then be expected to remember how to do 6 different tasks. Furthermore, having them hand-write answers on a printed worksheet (Q2-6) in a computer lab is a missed opportunity. They need to build muscle memory with the mouse and keyboard. The "Performance Challenge" (Q7) is excellent because it requires actual software manipulation, but the new safety skills are treated like a high school vocabulary test.

## Recommended restructure
Keep the 25-minute challenge and 25-minute post-test, but interleave the Tell/Show/Do for the new content.

*   **0–10m: Warm-up & Intro.** Discuss past skills. Hook: "Has anyone ever received a text about a package you didn't order?" State objectives.
*   **10–20m: Cycle 1 - Comfort & Accessibility.** 
    *   *Tell/Show:* Slides 2–4; Video 0:42. Instructor physically models adjusting a monitor.
    *   *Do/Review:* Learners adjust their station (WS Q1) and turn on Windows Live Captions or use `Tab` to navigate the browser.
*   **20–30m: Cycle 2 - Phishing & Verification.**
    *   *Tell/Show:* Slides 8–9; Video 1:27 & 2:09. 
    *   *Do/Review:* Learners open a new tab, search for a known local service (e.g., VA clinic), and bookmark the real site. (Replaces WS Q3).
*   **30–40m: Cycle 3 - Permissions & USBs.**
    *   *Tell/Show:* Slides 12–13; Video 3:38 & 4:19.
    *   *Do/Review:* Learners navigate to Chrome/Edge settings to view which sites have camera access. (Replaces WS Q5).
*   **40–45m: Cycle 4 - Passwords & Encryption.**
    *   *Tell/Show:* Slides 10–11; Video 2:50.
    *   *Do/Review:* Instructor demonstrates saving a Word doc with a password. (Replaces WS Q6).
*   **45–55m: Break.** (Screen-free).
*   **55–80m: Performance Challenge.** Worksheet Q7 (with the CSV formula error fixed). Instructor grades via rubric.
*   **80–105m: Post-test.** Independent work.
*   **105–120m: Review & Application.** Slides 19–20. Discuss results and next steps. 
*   *Note: Move FOMO/Catfishing (Slides 5-7) to Week 6 or treat as a brief discussion during the Warm-up, as it is behavioral and lacks a lab-based "Do" component.*

## Top 10 changes, ranked

1. **Fix the CSV formula error (Worksheet Q7 / Answer Guide Q7):** Change the source file from `supplies.csv` to `supplies.xlsx`, or change the instruction to "Calculate the total and type the number." *Why: Content correctness; formulas do not save in CSVs. Effort: Small.*
2. **Break the 20-minute presentation into short TSDR cycles (Lesson Plan):** Alternate 5 minutes of instruction with 5 minutes of practice. *Why: WIPPEA / Adult cognitive load. Effort: Medium.*
3. **Change Worksheet Q2–Q6 to hands-on tasks (Worksheet):** Instead of writing definitions, have learners perform actions (e.g., "Open browser settings and find the Camera permissions"). *Why: TSDR (Do must match the real-world skill). Effort: Medium.*
4. **Play video in chapters, not all at once (Lesson Plan):** Pause the video after each specific topic to allow for immediate practice. *Why: TSDR / Adult learning (chunking). Effort: Small.*
5. **Clarify Password vs. Encryption (Slide 10 / Video transcript):** Note that in modern document software, adding a "Password to Open" is usually what turns on the encryption. *Why: Content correctness. Effort: Small.*
6. **Model physical ergonomics (Lesson Plan):** During the workstation comfort segment, the instructor must physically demonstrate adjusting a chair/monitor, not just show the slide. *Why: TSDR (Show). Effort: Small.*
7. **Add "Example:" to the keyboard route (Slide 4):** Clarify that `Tab -> Tab -> Enter` is just one example of website navigation, not a universal rule. *Why: Content correctness. Effort: Small.*
8. **Make Universal Design practice tactile (Lesson Plan):** Have learners actually turn on Windows Live Captions (`Win + Ctrl + L` in Win 11) or use the Magnifier. *Why: TSDR (Do). Effort: Small.*
9. **Move FOMO/Catfishing to discussion (Lesson Plan):** Because these are behavioral concepts without a software "Do" step, move them to the Warm-up or Application discussion rather than taking up lab practice time. *Why: Flow / Orientation. Effort: Small.*
10. **Frame the Performance Challenge clearly (Lesson Plan / Slide 15):** Explicitly tell learners that the challenge covers *past* weeks' skills, so they don't get confused trying to apply today's safety lessons to the spreadsheet task. *Why: Adult learning (Orientation/Clarity). Effort: Small.*
