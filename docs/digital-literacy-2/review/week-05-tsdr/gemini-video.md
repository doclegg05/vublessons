# Week 5 video evaluation

## Verdict
The video is highly accurate, visually clear, and perfectly tailored to the emotional and practical needs of older adult learners, using excellent plain language and reassuring framing. However, the pacing of the embedded practice prompts is severely flawed, offering zero wait time before revealing answers, which will frustrate learners with low tech confidence. It is usable in class with instructor support, provided the instructor is prepared to manually pause the video at specific moments. Overall score: 4/5.

## Top issues, ranked

1.  **Severity: High** | **Timestamps: 2:43 and 6:14**
    *   **The problem:** There is zero wait time after the narrator asks a question before the answer is given.
    *   **Why it matters:** Older adults with low tech confidence cannot physically find and click the pause button fast enough. Giving the answer immediately ruins the retrieval practice and causes frustration.
    *   **Recommended fix:** instructor note (no edit) - The instructor must hover over the pause button and click it the exact second the prompt finishes.
2.  **Severity: Medium** | **Timestamps: 0:05 and 3:53**
    *   **The problem:** Confusing and inconsistent use of the word "Pause" in on-screen text. At 0:05, it accompanies a rhetorical question. At 3:53, it says "Pause - Keep the drive disconnected," using the word to mean "stop your physical action" rather than "pause the video."
    *   **Why it matters:** This audience takes literal cues from the screen. Inconsistent instructions will cause them to pause the video unnecessarily or become confused about what is expected of them.
    *   **Recommended fix:** re-edit visuals - Remove the word "Pause" at 0:05, and change the text at 3:53 to "Stop - Keep the drive disconnected."

*   **1:54:** Excellent plain-language advice ("A convincing logo is not proof") that demystifies technology for novices.
*   **2:14:** Screen demonstrations use a large cursor and a clear yellow highlight to indicate clicks, making it incredibly easy to follow.
*   **5:02:** The video addresses the emotional and attention-draining aspects of online safety, which is highly relevant and validating for this audience.
*   **7:05:** Reassuring, adult-appropriate framing of assessments ("A classroom score is one piece of evidence, not a judgment").

## Tips for the instructor using this video on Monday

*   **Keep your hand on the pause button!** The video does not provide built-in wait time. You must pause manually at 1:26, 2:43, 3:36, 5:00, and 6:14 to give learners time to think.
*   At **2:43**, after pausing, ask the class to vote by raising hands on which action is safer before playing the answer.
*   At **3:37**, hold up a real USB drive to physically demonstrate the scenario of finding an unknown device in a parking lot.
*   At **6:14**, after pausing, have learners work in pairs to state the three things (evidence, unverified, next step) before revealing the answer.
*   Tell learners to ignore the on-screen text that says "Pause" at **0:05** and **3:53**, as these are not actual video pause prompts.
*   Use the chapter break at **1:26** to check in with learners and ensure they are comfortable with their physical desk setup in the lab.
