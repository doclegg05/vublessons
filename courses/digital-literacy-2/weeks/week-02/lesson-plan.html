Week 2 lesson plan: Find, judge, and organize information

Course home

Find a reliable community resource and manage a shared file with care.

Learning outcomes

  • Refine a search and justify a source choice.
  • Complete a fictional online form and check its confirmation.
  • Organize files, set access and explain a recovery path.

Prepare the room

Provide a workstation and headphones for each learner, this lesson and worksheet, a word processor and spreadsheet app. Use instructor-provided fictional accounts or a modeled demonstration for cloud tasks. Test the local video and printer destination before class. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated. For week 6, supply a plain-text editor and the downloadable starter app; use an approved AI tool only if available.

Two-hour teaching plan

Minutes from startPhaseInstructor and learner actions
0–10 (10 min)ReconnectAsk learners to demonstrate last week’s check-one-change routine.
10–30 (20 min)Search demonstrationTeach keywords, filters and source evaluation with the supplied fictional source comparison.
30–50 (20 min)Research labLearners locate a public library service from an official source and record evidence. No account or application is needed.
50–60 (10 min)BreakPause away from screens.
60–80 (20 min)Files demonstrationTeach forms, central storage, sync, access, compression and version history. Play the cloud-file explainer.
80–110 (30 min)Files labUse the practice form and sharing simulator, then organize a fictional folder. Complete the worksheet.
110–120 (10 min)Evidence checkAsk each learner to justify a source and a permission. Use the knowledge checks.

Using the chaptered video

Use relevant chapters during the demonstration phases, within the existing time allocation. The full video provides approximately seven to eight minutes of instruction; paused practice uses the scheduled lab time. Ask learners to choose a familiar community or household task, predict the result before the demonstration, and explain the evidence afterward. Replay only the chapter needed for a refresher. Do not add the video as extra time on top of the two-hour plan.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Pair a driver and coach, then switch. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Write a focused question about a local public service.
  • Record an initial search and one refinement or filter. Explain its effect.
  • Record a source title, organization, URL, access date and useful detail.
  • Judge the source’s accuracy, perspective, bias, credibility and relevance. Name one check still needed.
  • Complete the fictional practice form. Record a validation message and the confirmation.
  • Create a folder and clear filename. Describe who controls a cloud copy.
  • Choose permissions for a reader, a reviewer and a coauthor. Demonstrate one in the simulator.
  • Explain when to use version history, a recycle bin or a backup. Explain why ZIP and read-only do not prove encryption.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
