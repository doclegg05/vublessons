Week 5 · Instructor notes

Slide 1

Protect your work. Show your skills.

Welcome learners by name. This lesson uses fictional examples and existing tools. Open the worksheet before demonstrations. F toggles fullscreen; N shows notes. Arrow keys progress through shows before advancing slides.

Slide 2

Start with what you know.

8 minutes including the welcome. Invite a real example without asking for private account or benefits details. Connect existing experience to today’s task.

Slide 3

Your four missions.

5 minutes. Preview the task sequence and success evidence. Use prepared examples if software or accounts are unavailable. The break is protected; optional extensions can wait.

Mission 5A. App: Workstation / browser. Explain why this helps: One useful adjustment you can reverse. Connect to the learner’s own household or community task. Ask for a prediction before the show. Full procedural steps and expected evidence are in the worksheet and answer guide.

Slide 7

Which design helps a learner who cannot use a mouse?

Ask everyone to choose before selecting an answer. Correct answer: Keyboard access with visible focus. Keyboard access and a visible focus indicator show where the next action will occur. Check the actual Point B evidence, not simply completion of a click. Misconception to address: the other options may look convenient but do not accomplish the stated task.

Slide 21

Show your progress.

22 minutes. Use the existing 20-question post-test, separate from five-task observations. No coaching during the test. Check the submission status and saved PDF. A saved PDF is not proof of online submission. Classroom results do not award certification.

Slide 22

Take one useful thing home.

Invite one specific transfer task, such as finding a service, saving a handout or checking a suspicious message. Do not require anyone to reveal personal information. Videos are optional replay resources; no additional viewing time is silently added to the 120-minute lesson.

Slide 23

Mission complete.

Collect evidence, not just self-reported confidence. Celebrate a concrete improvement. Close applications and restore shared settings. Keep support needs separate from assessment results.
