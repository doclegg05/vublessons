Slide 1

Opening video · Week 5

Allow about 26 minutes for this 8:10 video and its pauses. Ask learners to open the worksheet first. Press Play; it never starts automatically. The video stops at pause cards: each card holds 10 seconds of silence and names the worksheet tasks to do. Press Pause, let learners work, then press Play when most are done. Use the transcript link for chapter replay in a separate tab. Leaving this slide pauses playback. With the video focused, its native controls own the keyboard; Tab to Continue to lesson for deck navigation.

Slide 8

Round 2: Color is not the only clue

App: Worksheet · fictional volunteer schedule. Color or shading alone leaves out people who see color differently, people using a black-and-white printer, and anyone in poor light. A word or symbol beside the color fixes it. Keyboard access with a visible outline helps someone who cannot use a mouse. Worksheet section 5A-r2 (open worksheet.html#m5A-r2). If the room is slow, skip the Done early lines first, then the last round.

Slide 9

Round 3: Bigger, then back

App: Windows · Magnifier · Edge. Magnifier enlarges the whole screen; browser zoom enlarges one website. Both undo cleanly, which matters on a shared computer. Text size in Settings changes every app, so learners only look at it here. Worksheet section 5A-r3 (open worksheet.html#m5A-r3). If the room is slow, skip the Done early lines first, then the last round.

Which design helps a learner who cannot use a mouse?

Slide 14

Round 2: Four messages

App: Worksheet · four fictional messages. Common scams this year: unpaid-toll texts, pop-ups with a support phone number, a "grandchild" in trouble asking for gift cards. Microsoft error messages never include a phone number. Scam texts can be forwarded to 7726. The library notice gives no link and no pressure, so it is probably fine; check it on the library site you saved. Worksheet section 5B-r2 (open worksheet.html#m5B-r2). If the room is slow, skip the Done early lines first, then the last round.

Slide 15

Round 3: Never read back a code

App: Worksheet · fictional text message. A one-time code proves you are you. Anyone who asks you to read it back is trying to get into your account. A real bank will not ask. Call the number on the back of your card. Worksheet section 5B-r3 (open worksheet.html#m5B-r3). If the room is slow, skip the Done early lines first, then the last round.

Slide 20

Round 2: A password you will never use

App: Worksheet · four sample passwords. Use a passkey when a site offers one. Otherwise use a long, unique passphrase and turn on two-step verification; a browser password manager can remember it. Never write a real password on the worksheet. Worksheet section 5C-r2 (open worksheet.html#m5C-r2). If the room is slow, skip the Done early lines first, then the last round.

Slide 21

Round 3: Which protection fits?

App: Worksheet · five situations. Each protection does one job. On a shared lab login, sign out rather than lock. Device encryption protects a lost computer that is turned off; its recovery key is in the Microsoft account. Read-only limits edits but anyone can still read the file. Worksheet section 5C-r3 (open worksheet.html#m5C-r3). If the room is slow, skip the Done early lines first, then the last round.

Slide 22

Which makes file contents unreadable without the key?

Ask everyone to choose before selecting an answer. Correct answer: Encryption. Encryption protects readable data. Read-only restricts edits and ZIP packages files; neither replaces it. Check the actual Point B evidence, not simply completion of a click. Misconception to address: the other options may look convenient but do not accomplish the stated task.

Show your progress.

22 minutes. Use the existing 20-question post-test, separate from five-task observations. No coaching during the test. Check the submission status and saved PDF. A saved PDF is not proof of online submission. Classroom results do not award certification.

Slide 28

Take one useful thing home.

Invite one specific transfer task, such as finding a service, saving a handout or checking a suspicious message. Do not require anyone to reveal personal information. Videos are optional replay resources; no additional viewing time is silently added to the 120-minute lesson.

Slide 29

Mission complete.

Collect evidence, not just self-reported confidence. Celebrate a concrete improvement. Close applications and restore shared settings. Keep support needs separate from assessment results.
