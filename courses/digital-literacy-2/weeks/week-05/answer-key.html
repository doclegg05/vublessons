Week 5 · Answer and observation guide

Assess observable evidence. A spoken prompt is “With prompt”; using the quick card alone can be independent. Mark simulated work separately from hands-on performance. These observations do not change assessment scores.

Mission 5A · Make your station comfortable

  1. A specific change (chair, screen about an arm’s length away with the top at or slightly below eye level, text size or Magnifier) and a benefit the learner can see; no medical claims. Settings restored.
  2. Universal design makes tools usable by more people without special changes. Examples: captions (noisy room, hearing loss), keyboard access (cannot use a mouse), clear text labels that do not rely on color.

Round 2 · Color is not the only clue

  1. Set up chairs: Full. Help desk: One spot left. Clean up: Open. Phone reminders: Full. A fix for someone who cannot use a mouse: every control works with the Tab key and shows a visible outline.

Round 3 · Bigger, then back

  1. Magnifier (Windows and plus) enlarges everything on the screen; Windows and Esc closes it. Ctrl and plus in Edge enlarges only that website; Ctrl and 0 puts it back.

Review: Keyboard access and a visible focus outline show where the next action will happen.

Mission 5B · Pause and verify independently

  1. Signs: pressure to act now, a sender or link that does not match the organization (account-check.example), a request for a password. Check: the real website from a bookmark or typed address, or a number from a card or letter, never the message’s link or number. If a password was typed: change it on the real site, turn on two-step verification and tell the instructor.
  2. (a) Possible catfishing: send no money and keep no secrets; verify another way. (b) Fear of missing out: set a stopping point or quiet alerts with Do not disturb. (c) Do not reply in anger; keep screenshots if safe; report or block; talk to someone you trust.

Round 2 · Four messages

  1. Toll text: pressure and a strange link; use the toll agency's own site or app you already know, and forward the text to 7726. Pop-up: a phone number in an error message is a scam; close the browser and ask staff. Grandchild call: secrecy and gift cards; hang up and call family on a number you know. Library email: no pressure and no link; check your account on the library site you saved.

Round 3 · Never read back a code

  1. Nothing in the text can be verified: the sender, the charge and the story. A bank never asks you to read back a code. Do not reply; call the number on your card. If the code was shared, call the bank now and change the password.

Review: Pressure for money and secrecy call for an independent check. A profile photo is not proof of identity.

Mission 5C · Protect access and recovery

  1. USB: do not connect; give it to staff. Camera: deny when the task does not need it. Benefits fee: do not pay or use the link; VA never charges a fee to apply; call a number from VA.gov. Windows 10: free security updates ended October 2025; use Extended Security Updates or a supported device. One real detail from each page; nothing changed or installed.
  2. Encrypt with Password sets a password to open and encrypts the file; Word cannot recover a lost password. Always Open Read-Only and Restrict Editing only limit changes; the text is still readable. Device encryption protects a lost or stolen computer that is turned off; lock the screen with Windows logo key and L before stepping away.

Round 2 · A password you will never use

  1. Weakest to strongest: password123, Beckley1, Tr0ub4dor!, maple-river-lantern-cocoa. A good passphrase is four unrelated words, used for one account only. Second check: a code from an app or a text, or a passkey.

Round 3 · Which protection fits?

  1. Library computer: sign out. Lost laptop: device encryption (recovery key kept in the Microsoft account). Flyer: read-only. Private letter: password to open, which encrypts it. Laptop might stop: a backup copy somewhere else.

Review: Encryption protects readable data. Read-only restricts edits and ZIP packages files; neither replaces it.

Mission 5D · Show what you can do

  1. Rate each row Independent, With prompt or Needs practice on the paper roster, using the evidence list below. The quick card counts as Independent; any spoken prompt is With prompt. Keep this separate from the post-test score.
  2. Complete PDF saved or printed; score out of 20 on the roster; a named strength and a specific next task tied to a results domain.

Review: Record the support used honestly. The observation and the scored post-test stay separate.

Five-task observation evidence

  • Refine a search: The question names the place and the need, and the learner opens the organization’s own page, not just a summary, and reads the hours there. A specific place and time make the results more useful.
  • Build a structured paragraph: The title uses Heading 1, the steps are a Word numbered list (select one number and all three highlight), and the warning line is bold.
  • Total four costs: B6 shows 29 and the formula bar shows =SUM(B2:B5) or =B2+B3+B4+B5, not a typed number. Check before the file is closed: a .csv file keeps the result, not the formula.
  • Bring back deleted text: Presses Ctrl and Z (or Undo) while the file is still open and the line returns; says Undo is lost once the file closes, and that version history works for files saved in OneDrive.
  • Write useful feedback: Names at least two missing details (a.m. or p.m., the date, the room), why a reader needs them, and one specific, polite fix.
