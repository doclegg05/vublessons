Week 5 lesson plan: Protect your work and show your skills

Course home

Practice safe decisions, complete a skills challenge, and measure your growth.

Learning outcomes

  • Comfort and access: at a lab workstation, each learner makes one comfort change (chair, screen distance, text size or Magnifier) and says what it made easier, then names two universal design features and who each one helps. The change is visible, and lab settings are restored afterwards.
  • Scams: given the fictional message on slide 8, each learner names two warning signs and an independent check that does not use the message’s link or number, says what to do after typing a password, and gives a safe next step for a catfishing request, fear of missing out and a hurtful post, with no more than one prompt.
  • Devices and files: using slide 9, Edge settings, Windows Update and Word’s Protect Document menu without changing anything, each learner chooses safe responses to an unknown USB drive and an unneeded camera request, says why updates matter, tells a password to open (encryption) apart from read-only, and says what device encryption protects and when to lock the screen. Both knowledge checks are answered correctly.
  • Skills challenge and post-test: working alone, each learner completes the five challenge rows (refined search, structured paragraph, formula total, undo recovery, useful feedback), each rated Independent, With prompt or Needs practice, then completes the 20-question post-test and records the score and one next practice task.

Prepare the room

Each station: Microsoft Edge, Word and Excel, and this lesson and worksheet; check that a downloaded .csv file opens in Excel. At the front: the projector with Edge, Word and Windows Settings, and a USB drive to hold up for slide 12 (never plug it in). Print a paper roster with each learner code, five challenge columns, the week 1 pre-test score and a post-test column, and bring the week 1 scores. The lab needs no accounts: the phishing message, camera prompt and file protections are course simulations or look-only steps, and two-step verification is a home task. Ask IT before class whether the lab’s Windows 10 computers get Extended Security Updates, because learners will ask. Check that Windows Update, Edge site permissions and Word’s Protect Document menu open under a learner login. Play chapters 4 and 7 with captions to check sound. After your own run-through, delete any help-desk.docx and supplies files you created and select Start fresh on this computer at each station. Keep 3–5 printed post-tests in case the network fails. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated.

Lab quick card: Windows 11

Click-by-click steps for this week’s tasks in Windows 11 with Microsoft Edge. If your screen looks different, ask your instructor.

To do thisSteps
Zoom in with MagnifierPress Windows logo key and + to zoom in. Press Windows logo key and Esc to close Magnifier.
Quiet your notificationsStart › Settings › System › Notifications. Turn off apps you do not need. For a quiet hour, open the notification center and select the bell with zZ to turn on Do not disturb.
See where a link really goesPoint to the link without clicking and read the web address that appears. If it does not match the organization’s real website, do not open it.
Report a phishing emailOutlook.com: select the message, then Report › Report phishing. Gmail: open the message, select More (⋮) next to Reply, then Report phishing.
Turn on two-step verification (at home)Microsoft account: go to account.microsoft.com/security, select Manage how I sign in, then under Additional security, Two-step verification, select Turn on.
Check for Windows updatesStart › Settings › Windows Update › Check for updates. If updates are listed, select Download & install. Windows 10: Start › Settings › Update & Security › Windows Update.
Review which websites can use your cameraIn Edge, open Settings from the ··· menu and type permissions in the Search settings box. Open Camera to see which sites are allowed; select a site to change it to Block.
Lock the computer when you step awayPress Windows logo key and L. Sign in again when you come back.
Require a password to open a Word fileFile › Info › Protect Document › Encrypt with Password. Type the password twice, then save. Word cannot recover a lost password.
Stop changes but not readingFile › Info › Protect Document › Always Open Read-Only, or Review › Restrict Editing. Anyone can still read and copy the text.
Check device encryptionStart › Settings › Privacy & security › Device encryption. If it is missing, the computer may not support it or you may be signed in as a standard user.
Bring back something you just deletedPress Ctrl and Z right away. It works after you save, until you close the file. For a file saved in OneDrive, select the file name at the top, then Version history.
Total a column in ExcelSelect the empty cell under the numbers, type =SUM(B2:B5) and press Enter. Select the cell again to see the formula in the formula bar.

Using the chaptered video

Play each chapter inside its cycle, as the Show, from the video slide. Pause at the times named in the plan; the narration does not stop by itself. Ask learners to predict before a demonstration and explain the evidence afterward. Replay only the chapter needed for a refresher.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Every learner works at their own workstation; a partner coaches but does not take over. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Adjust one thing at your station so you can read without leaning in: chair height, screen distance (about an arm’s length) or text size. Or try Magnifier: press Windows logo key and +, then Windows logo key and Esc to close it. Write what you changed and what got easier, then put it back.
  • Play the first 10 seconds of the slide 14 video with the sound muted and read the captions. Then press Tab a few times and watch the outline move from control to control. Name two design features that help more people, and who each one helps.
  • On slide 8, select the subject, the sender and the link to inspect each one. Write two warning signs, the independent check you would use instead (a real website or a number you already trust), and what you would do if you had already typed your password.
  • Name the risk and one safe next step for each: (a) a new online friend asks you to keep a money request secret; (b) you stay up late scrolling because you do not want to miss anything; (c) someone posts something hurtful about you in a public group.
  • Say your choice for each card on slide 9 before you flip it. Then look without changing anything: in Edge, open Settings from the ··· menu, search permissions and open Camera; in Windows 10, open Start › Settings › Update & Security › Windows Update (Windows 11: Start › Settings › Windows Update). Write one thing each page shows.
  • In Word, open a blank document and select File › Info › Protect Document. Write which option asks for a password to open and encrypts the file, and which only limits changes. Do not set a password. Then write what device encryption protects and what you do before you step away from a signed-in computer.
  • Skills challenge, on your own: refine a search, build a structured paragraph, total four costs with a formula, bring back deleted text and write useful feedback. Leave your work on screen for your instructor.
  • Take the post-test on your own. Print page 1 of your results or save it as a PDF, and write your score out of 28. Name one strength and one skill to practice next.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
