Week 5 lesson plan: Protect your work and show your skills

Course home

Practice safe decisions, complete a skills challenge, and measure your growth.

Learning outcomes

  • Explain device, file and account protections.
  • Recognize manipulation and choose a safe next step.
  • Demonstrate core tasks and complete the parallel post-test.

Prepare the room

Provide a workstation and headphones for each learner, this lesson and worksheet, a word processor and spreadsheet app. Use instructor-provided fictional accounts or a modeled demonstration for cloud tasks. Test the local video and printer destination before class. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated. For week 6, supply a plain-text editor and the downloadable starter app; use an approved AI tool only if available.

Two-hour teaching plan

Minutes from startPhaseInstructor and learner actions
0–10 (10 min)Retrieve and previewAsk learners to name a source check, a file permission and a useful feedback habit.
10–30 (20 min)Safety modelTeach ergonomics, universal design, manipulation, encryption, unknown media and camera permissions.
30–45 (15 min)Scenario practiceUse the flip cards and explainer. Learners state the evidence and safest next step.
45–55 (10 min)BreakTake a screen-free break.
55–80 (25 min)Performance challengeLearners demonstrate the core tasks on the worksheet. Instructor records independent, prompted or needs practice.
80–105 (25 min)Post-testComplete the parallel assessment independently. Enter the saved pre-test score only if the learner wants a comparison. Print or save results.
105–120 (15 min)Review and next stepsDiscuss explanations after submission. Choose a practice priority without treating the classroom score as certification.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Pair a driver and coach, then switch. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Adjust one workstation feature and explain the need it addresses.
  • Define universal design and name two useful examples.
  • For an urgent suspicious message, write an independent verification plan.
  • Explain catfishing, FOMO and a constructive response to harmful posts.
  • Choose responses to unknown USB media and an unnecessary camera request.
  • Explain device encryption, a file password and editing restrictions without treating them as identical.
  • Performance challenge: refine a public-service search; justify a source; create a structured paragraph; calculate a three-item total; choose sharing access; give useful feedback; name a recovery method.
  • Save or print your post-test results. Identify one strength and one next practice task.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
