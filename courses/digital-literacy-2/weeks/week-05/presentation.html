Universal design helps more people

Universal design means building tools that more people can use without special changes. Captions help in a noisy room or with hearing loss. Keyboard access helps people who cannot use a mouse, and clear text labels help people who cannot tell some colors apart.

An online identity can be invented

Catfishing is using a fake identity to build trust, sometimes with stolen photos of real service members. Pressure for money, secrecy or personal information is a reason to pause and verify another way. Never send money or gift cards to someone you have not met in person.

An urgent message is not proof

Phishing is a fake message posing as a trusted organization. Warning signs: pressure to act now, a mismatched sender or link, a request for a password or money. Check through the real website or a number you trust. Typed a password already? Change it and turn on two-step verification.

Encryption protects readable data

Encryption scrambles data so only someone with the key can read it. Device encryption (BitLocker in Windows, FileVault on a Mac) protects a lost computer that is turned off. A signed-in computer is open to anyone at the keyboard, so press Windows logo key and L when you step away.

Passwords protect different actions

In Word, Encrypt with Password sets a password to open and encrypts the file. Always Open Read-Only and Restrict Editing only limit changes; anyone can still read the text. Word cannot recover a lost password, so keep it somewhere safe, away from the file.

Unknown devices can carry risk

An unknown USB drive can carry malware: software made to harm a computer or steal information. Do not plug it in to find its owner; give it to lab staff. Keep Windows and your browser updated, because updates fix known security holes.
