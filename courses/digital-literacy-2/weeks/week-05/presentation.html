Week 5XWorking in: Microsoft ExcelPart 1 · Why it matters

Do not let color do all the work.

  1. 1
    Some people cannot tell red from green. A black-and-white printer shows both as gray.
  2. 2
    Add words next to the color, such as Complete and Not yet. Now the sheet works for everyone.
  3. 3
    The same goes for charts: add labels or patterns to the bars, so the meaning does not depend on color alone.

Do not let color do all the work.

Ask: has anyone had trouble reading something because of its colors? About one man in twelve cannot tell red from green. A printed copy loses color too. Tonight we color a sheet, then fix it. The same fix works on charts.

Week 5eWorking in: Microsoft EdgePart 2 · Why it matters

Two locks are better than one.

  1. 1
    A long password is stronger than a short one. Four words in a row is long and easy to remember.
  2. 2
    Use it on one site only. If one site loses your password, a reused one opens your other accounts too.
  3. 3
    Two-step verification is a second lock: after your password, the site sends a code to your phone.

Two locks are better than one.

Ask: how many sites could one reused password open? The practice passphrase is for this page only. Real passphrases are never shown on a slide, shared or written on the result page.

Week 5MWorking in: Practice MailPart 3 · Why it matters

Pressure is a warning sign.

  1. 1
    Words like act now and stops today try to make you hurry. Hurrying is how people get tricked.
  2. 2
    A link can say one thing and go somewhere else. Point at it, without clicking, and read the real address.
  3. 3
    Check through a route you already trust: a number on a letter or card you already have, or a website you saved. Never the message’s own link or number.

Pressure is a warning sign.

Ask: has anyone had a message or call that said hurry? Pressure is the clue, not proof. The same habit works for a phone call: hang up, then call the number you already trust.
