Safety is a set of decisions

Choose one habit that protects your work and one that helps you use technology comfortably.

A comfortable workstation

Place the screen where you can read without strain. Support your posture, take breaks and adjust input devices to your needs. Ask for help with persistent discomfort.

Universal design helps more people

Captions, readable controls and flexible input methods make tools usable in more situations. Keyboard access helps people who cannot use a mouse.

Protect attention and wellbeing

Frequent alerts and comparison can create pressure. FOMO means fear of missing out. Adjust notifications and choose a stopping point.

An online identity can be invented

Catfishing uses a false identity to build trust. Pressure for money, secrecy or personal information is a reason to pause and verify independently.

Respond to harmful posts

Avoid escalating. Preserve evidence when safe, use reporting or blocking tools and seek support. Negative posts can affect real relationships and future opportunities.

Resource desk · Room A

Readable and editable.

Compare what someone can read with what they can change. The encrypted text is illustrative, not a real encryption tool.

Read the explanation

Encryption scrambles data so a key is needed to read it. Device encryption can protect a lost powered-off device; an unlocked signed-in session still needs protection.

Passwords protect different actions

Some file passwords control opening; others restrict editing. Check which protection is enabled and how recovery works. Avoid storing the password beside the protected file.

Unknown devices can carry risk

Portable storage may contain malicious software. Use approved devices and follow lab procedures. Do not plug in unknown media to find its owner.
