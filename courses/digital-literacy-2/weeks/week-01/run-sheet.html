Cycle A: Readable text minute 38–50 · 12 min

Objective: Make text readable, then restore it. post 1, pre 1

Pose the problem: This page is too small to read. What do you change, and how do you get back?

Tell

  • Two different controls fix small text. Browser zoom (Ctrl and +) makes one website bigger, and Edge remembers that zoom for that site only.
  • Display scaling, in Windows Settings, makes everything bigger: every app, every menu, every button.
  • Pick the control that matches the problem. One page too small: zoom. Everything too small: scaling.
  • Always know the way back: Ctrl and 0 resets zoom; the scale menu goes back to 100%.

Show (you, on the projector)

  1. In Edge, open this lesson. Press Ctrl and + twice. Point at the zoom bubble (110%, then 125%) and at the Next slide button: it grew too.
  2. Press Ctrl and 0. The page returns to 100%.
  3. Open the course home in a new tab. It is not zoomed: Edge keeps zoom per site.
  4. Start › Settings › System › Display. Under Scale and layout, open the Scale menu (100%, 125%). Say: this changes every app on this computer; I am not changing the lab PC. Close Settings.

Do (every learner, own seat)

Worksheet task 2, every learner at their own seat: press Ctrl and + until this lesson is comfortable to read, find and select Next slide, then press Ctrl and 0 to reset. Write down the zoom you used.

Review

  • A partner confirms the page is back at 100% (no zoom bubble).
  • Ask the room: "You zoomed a webpage, but Word and your email are still too small. What do you change?" Answer: display scaling, then check each app.

Slides: Make text comfortable → Zoom, then scale → Practice: zoom without losing your place

Cycle B: Sound in the right place minute 50–58 · 8 min

Objective: Send sound to the right device. IC3 1.4

Pose the problem: A video is playing. Nobody hears it. Where did the sound go?

Tell

  • Sound goes to one chosen device: the speakers, a headset or the monitor.
  • Three checks, in order: is it muted, which output is selected, then the volume. Change one thing, then test.
  • Brightness and contrast live on the monitor’s own buttons on a desktop computer. Point to them; do not change them today.

Show (you, on the projector)

  1. Select the speaker icon at the right end of the taskbar. Point at the mute symbol beside the slider; select it to mute, then again to unmute.
  2. Select the device name above the slider (the ˄ arrow on Windows 11). Choose the headset, then choose the speakers again.
  3. Play the first ten seconds of the Week 1 video on slide 21 as the test sound.
  4. Point at the buttons on the front of the monitor: brightness lives there.

Do (every learner, own seat)

Worksheet task 3, every learner: put on the headset, choose it as the output, play the first ten seconds of the video on slide 21, then choose the speakers again.

Review

  • Slide 9 knowledge check: the video is playing silently. What do you check first? (Output device and mute.)

Slides: Sound and screen checks → Knowledge check: the wrong speaker

Cycle C: One good page minute 68–80 · 12 min

Objective: Set up one good page in print preview. post 4, pre 4

Pose the problem: You need twenty flyers. The preview shows the last line alone on page 2. What now?

Tell

  • The preview is the test. Before Print, check four things: the printer name, the page count, orientation, and Pages and Copies.
  • Windows and Edge often pick the last printer someone used. Read the name every time.
  • If a line spills onto a new page, fix the layout (margins, text size), preview again, and only then print one test page.
  • Never press Print twice. If nothing happens, open the queue and ask.

Show (you, on the projector)

  1. Open the worksheet in Edge and press Ctrl and P. Point at: the Printer menu, the page count, Layout (portrait or landscape), Pages and Copies.
  2. Set Pages to 1 and Copies to 1. Say why: one test sheet, not twenty.
  3. Show the spill: the last line on its own page. Open More settings › Margins › Narrow. The page count drops; the preview changes.
  4. Select Cancel. There is no printer today, so the preview is the whole job.
  5. Slide 12: turn each connection card with the room. HDMI carries the picture, Ethernet the network, USB data or power.

Do (every learner, own seat)

Worksheet task 4, every learner: press Ctrl and P on page 1 of this worksheet. Write the printer name, orientation and page count. Set Pages to 1 and Copies to 1. Select Cancel. Then task 5: match the three cables and find one real port.

Review

  • A partner reads back the printer name and the page setting.
  • Ask: "The last line spills to page 2. Print page 1 only, or fix the layout and preview again?" (Fix it, preview, one test page.)

Slides: Printer choices → Print one page before twenty → Match a connection to its job

Cycle D: A calendar you can trust minute 80–95 · 15 min

Objective: Make a calendar entry that you and others can act on. post 2, post 3, pre 2

Pose the problem: Two clinic visits on different days, and a reminder that went off on the wrong day. How do you see the week at a glance, and how do you fix the reminder?

Tell

  • Five fields make an entry usable: title, day, start, end and place. Add a reminder early enough to help.
  • A repeat copies the event forward. If a reminder fires on the wrong day, fix the repeat settings and check the next occurrence.
  • Week view puts several days side by side, so you can spot conflicts. Day view shows detail; Month view the big picture.
  • Sharing free/busy shows other people only "Busy": no title, no place.

Show (you, on the projector)

  1. On your own calendar, select New event. Fill the five fields: Library practice, next Monday, 2:00 to 3:00 p.m., Room A. Add a 30-minute reminder. Save.
  2. Switch to Week view. Point at the entry and at the days either side of it.
  3. Open the event and set Repeat: weekly. Save, then find the next Monday. Say: if the reminder came on the wrong day, this is where you check.
  4. Share the calendar with "Can view when I’m busy" (Outlook) or "See only free/busy" (Google). Show what the other person sees: Busy, nothing else.

Do (every learner, own seat)

Worksheet task 6, every learner on paper, marked simulated: write the five fields, a reminder and a weekly repeat. Then task 7 on slides 14 and 15: choose Week view and say what it shows; choose free/busy and read what the partner sees.

Review

  • Slide 16 knowledge check: the partner only needs to know when you are free.
  • A partner checks the five fields on the paper entry.
  • Ask: "A Monday reminder keeps showing on Tuesday. What do you fix?" (The repeat settings; then check the next occurrence.)

Slides: A calendar entry needs enough detail → Switch the calendar view → Share availability thoughtfully → Knowledge check: calendar privacy

Cycle E: Check it, then ask well minute 95–107 · 12 min

Objective: Check an automatic change, and ask for help clearly. post 8, pre 3, pre 8

Pose the problem: Word changed a word you typed. Later the screen only says "folder unavailable". What do you do in each case?

Tell

  • The computer guesses for you: AutoCorrect, autocomplete, mail rules, cloud saving. Read what changed before you accept it.
  • Ctrl and Z undoes the last change right away.
  • When you are stuck, a good help request has four parts: the app and its version, what you were trying to do, what you already tried, and the exact message on the screen.
  • Use the app’s own Help or the maker’s support site, so the steps match your version.

Show (you, on the projector)

  1. In Word, type teh and a space. Watch it become the. Press Ctrl and Z: teh is back.
  2. Type a name such as Maren and a space. If Word changes it, undo it the same way.
  3. Press F1 or select Help and search for print one page. Point out the Word version in the results.
  4. On the projector, write a four-part help request for the print problem: "Word 2016. I am trying to print page 1 of the worksheet. I chose the training-room printer and pressed Print. It says Printer unavailable."

Do (every learner, own seat)

Worksheet task 8, every learner: type teh in Word and undo it with Ctrl and Z. Then write a four-part help request for the "folder unavailable" problem. A partner suggests one specific improvement.

Review

  • A partner names the missing part, if any: app and version, task, what you tried, exact message.
  • Ask: "Which request can a helper act on: ‘it doesn’t work’, or the four-part one?"

Slides: Automation still needs a check → Undo it, then ask well → Practice: write the help request
