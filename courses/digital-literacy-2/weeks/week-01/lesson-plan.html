Week 1 lesson plan: Make technology work for you

Course home

Set up a comfortable workspace, organize a calendar, and solve everyday device problems.

Learning outcomes

  • Adjust device and browser settings for a task.
  • Create a practice appointment and choose what others can see.
  • Select a connection and a printer; verify the result.

Prepare the room

Provide a workstation and headphones for each learner, this lesson and worksheet, a word processor and spreadsheet app. Use instructor-provided fictional accounts or a modeled demonstration for cloud tasks. Test the local video and printer destination before class. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated. For week 6, supply a plain-text editor and the downloadable starter app; use an approved AI tool only if available.

Two-hour teaching plan

Minutes from startPhaseInstructor and learner actions
0–10 (10 min)Welcome and goalsAsk which everyday task each learner wants to do more independently. Demonstrate text size and lesson navigation.
10–35 (25 min)Pre-testOpen the pre-test before instruction. Allow quiet work; read directions aloud without suggesting answers. Save or print each result.
35–55 (20 min)Model the setupTeach slides on display, sound, browser settings, processing, cables and printing. Think aloud through one change and its check.
55–65 (10 min)BreakOffer a screen-free pause.
65–80 (15 min)Calendar and automationModel a fictional appointment, free/busy sharing and an automation check. Play the explainer and pause for a prediction.
80–110 (30 min)Guided labPairs complete the workstation and calendar worksheet. Partners switch driver and coach roles halfway.
110–120 (10 min)Check and transferUse both knowledge checks. Each learner demonstrates one adjustment, explains why it helps and restores the lab setting.

Using the chaptered video

Use relevant chapters during the demonstration phases, within the existing time allocation. The full video provides approximately seven to eight minutes of instruction; paused practice uses the scheduled lab time. Ask learners to choose a familiar community or household task, predict the result before the demonstration, and explain the evidence afterward. Replay only the chapter needed for a refresher. Do not add the video as extra time on top of the two-hour plan.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Pair a driver and coach, then switch. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Record one task you want to make easier and one skill you bring.
  • Increase webpage text. Record the control used and how to reset it.
  • Select the correct audio output; explain brightness versus contrast.
  • Match HDMI, Ethernet and USB to three tasks. Check one actual device port.
  • Create a fictional library appointment with a reminder. Record the fields you checked.
  • Choose a calendar view and a sharing level for a partner who only needs availability. Explain why.
  • Preview a one-page handout; record the printer, orientation and page range. Do not print unless the instructor directs it.
  • Type a sentence with a name that autocorrect changes. Correct it. Record a useful help search and one piece of partner feedback.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
