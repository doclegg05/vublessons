Week 1 lesson plan: Make technology work for you

Course home

Set up a comfortable workspace, organize a calendar, and solve everyday device problems.

Learning outcomes

  • Settings: at a lab workstation with Edge, each learner enlarges a webpage with browser zoom and returns it to 100% (Ctrl and 0), selects the headset as the sound output and confirms it with a test, and names which control (page zoom, display scale or brightness) fits a described reading problem. All three are correct with no more than one prompt, and lab settings are restored.
  • Calendar: on the paper form (marked simulated) and the slide simulations, each learner writes a fictional event with title, day, start, end, place, a reminder and a repeat, chooses Week view to compare days, and chooses free/busy sharing for a partner, explaining what the partner will see.
  • Printing: each learner opens print preview for page 1 of the worksheet, confirms the printer name, records orientation and page count, and sets Pages to 1 and Copies to 1 without printing; given three jobs (external screen, wired network, flash drive), each learner matches HDMI, Ethernet and USB.
  • Automation: in Word, each learner triggers an AutoCorrect change (teh to the) and undoes it with Ctrl and Z.
  • Help: each learner writes a help request that names the app and version, the task, what they tried and the exact message, and records one specific piece of partner feedback.

Prepare the room

Each station: a headset, Microsoft Edge, Microsoft Word, and this lesson and worksheet open. At the front: the projector showing Edge, your own Outlook or Google calendar signed in for the calendar demonstration, and an HDMI, a USB and an Ethernet cable to hold up. No printer is available, so every print task stops at the preview. The lab has no calendar account, so worksheet task 6 is on paper. Print the run sheet. After your own run-through, select Start fresh on this computer at each station. Keep 3–5 printed pre-tests in case the network fails. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated.

Two-hour teaching plan

Minutes from startPhaseInstructor and learner actions
0–10 (10 min)Warm-up and goalsLearners sign in. Show the text-size button and Next / Previous on the lesson page. Hold up an HDMI, a USB and an Ethernet cable. Ask: what did each one carry in Level 1? (Picture, data or power, network.) Slide 2: pairs share one task they do well and one they want to make easier. Every learner writes worksheet task 1. Slide 1: read the five goals aloud. Say that the pre-test comes first and that nobody is expected to know it yet.
10–35 (25 min)Pre-testSlide 3. Learners open the pre-test and type their name. Read the directions aloud. Do not hint at answers. Record each score out of 28 on your roster. Print page 1 of a result only if someone needs a paper copy. Early finishers read slide 4. Late finishers may run to minute 38.
35–38 (3 min)The routineSlide 4. Say the three words and point at them. Tell learners they will use the same three words to check each other.
38–50 (12 min)Cycle A: Readable textPose the problem: This page is too small to read. What do you change, and how do you get back? Tell: Two different controls fix small text. Browser zoom (Ctrl and +) makes one website bigger, and Edge remembers that zoom for that site only. Show: In Edge, open this lesson. Press Ctrl and + twice. Point at the zoom bubble (110%, then 125%) and at the Next slide button: it grew too. Press Ctrl and 0. The page returns to 100%. Open the course home in a new tab. It is not zoomed: Edge keeps zoom per site. Start › Settings › System › Display. Under Scale and layout, open the Scale menu (100%, 125%). Say: this changes every app on this computer; I am not changing the lab PC. Close Settings. Do: Worksheet task 2, every learner at their own seat: press Ctrl and + until this lesson is comfortable to read, find and select Next slide, then press Ctrl and 0 to reset. Write down the zoom you used. Review: A partner confirms the page is back at 100% (no zoom bubble). Ask the room: "You zoomed a webpage, but Word and your email are still too small. What do you change?" Answer: display scaling, then check each app. Slides: Make text comfortable → Practice: zoom without losing your place.
50–58 (8 min)Cycle B: Sound in the right placePose the problem: A video is playing. Nobody hears it. Where did the sound go? Tell: Sound goes to one chosen device: the speakers, a headset or the monitor. Show: Select the speaker icon at the right end of the taskbar. Point at the mute symbol beside the slider; select it to mute, then again to unmute. Select the device name above the slider (the ˄ arrow on Windows 11). Choose the headset, then choose the speakers again. Play the first ten seconds of the Week 1 video on slide 21 as the test sound. Point at the buttons on the front of the monitor: brightness lives there. Do: Worksheet task 3, every learner: put on the headset, choose it as the output, play the first ten seconds of the video on slide 21, then choose the speakers again. Review: Slide 9 knowledge check: the video is playing silently. What do you check first? (Output device and mute.) Slides: Sound and screen checks → Knowledge check: the wrong speaker.
58–68 (10 min)BreakLeave the lesson page open on slide 10 for the return.
68–80 (12 min)Cycle C: One good pagePose the problem: You need twenty flyers. The preview shows the last line alone on page 2. What now? Tell: The preview is the test. Before Print, check four things: the printer name, the page count, orientation, and Pages and Copies. Show: Open the worksheet in Edge and press Ctrl and P. Point at: the Printer menu, the page count, Layout (portrait or landscape), Pages and Copies. Set Pages to 1 and Copies to 1. Say why: one test sheet, not twenty. Show the spill: the last line on its own page. Open More settings › Margins › Narrow. The page count drops; the preview changes. Select Cancel. There is no printer today, so the preview is the whole job. Slide 12: turn each connection card with the room. HDMI carries the picture, Ethernet the network, USB data or power. Do: Worksheet task 4, every learner: press Ctrl and P on page 1 of this worksheet. Write the printer name, orientation and page count. Set Pages to 1 and Copies to 1. Select Cancel. Then task 5: match the three cables and find one real port. Review: A partner reads back the printer name and the page setting. Ask: "The last line spills to page 2. Print page 1 only, or fix the layout and preview again?" (Fix it, preview, one test page.) Slides: Printer choices → Match a connection to its job.
80–95 (15 min)Cycle D: A calendar you can trustPose the problem: Two clinic visits on different days, and a reminder that went off on the wrong day. How do you see the week at a glance, and how do you fix the reminder? Tell: Five fields make an entry usable: title, day, start, end and place. Add a reminder early enough to help. Show: On your own calendar, select New event. Fill the five fields: Library practice, next Monday, 2:00 to 3:00 p.m., Room A. Add a 30-minute reminder. Save. Switch to Week view. Point at the entry and at the days either side of it. Open the event and set Repeat: weekly. Save, then find the next Monday. Say: if the reminder came on the wrong day, this is where you check. Share the calendar with "Can view when I’m busy" (Outlook) or "See only free/busy" (Google). Show what the other person sees: Busy, nothing else. Do: Worksheet task 6, every learner on paper, marked simulated: write the five fields, a reminder and a weekly repeat. Then task 7 on slides 14 and 15: choose Week view and say what it shows; choose free/busy and read what the partner sees. Review: Slide 16 knowledge check: the partner only needs to know when you are free. A partner checks the five fields on the paper entry. Ask: "A Monday reminder keeps showing on Tuesday. What do you fix?" (The repeat settings; then check the next occurrence.) Slides: A calendar entry needs enough detail → Knowledge check: calendar privacy.
95–107 (12 min)Cycle E: Check it, then ask wellPose the problem: Word changed a word you typed. Later the screen only says "folder unavailable". What do you do in each case? Tell: The computer guesses for you: AutoCorrect, autocomplete, mail rules, cloud saving. Read what changed before you accept it. Show: In Word, type teh and a space. Watch it become the. Press Ctrl and Z: teh is back. Type a name such as Maren and a space. If Word changes it, undo it the same way. Press F1 or select Help and search for print one page. Point out the Word version in the results. On the projector, write a four-part help request for the print problem: "Word 2016. I am trying to print page 1 of the worksheet. I chose the training-room printer and pressed Print. It says Printer unavailable." Do: Worksheet task 8, every learner: type teh in Word and undo it with Ctrl and Z. Then write a four-part help request for the "folder unavailable" problem. A partner suggests one specific improvement. Review: A partner names the missing part, if any: app and version, task, what you tried, exact message. Ask: "Which request can a helper act on: ‘it doesn’t work’, or the four-part one?" Slides: Automation still needs a check → Practice: write the help request.
107–115 (8 min)Individual checkSlide 20. Each learner shows the three things at their own seat. Mark Independent, With prompt or Needs practice on the roster. Re-model only the missing step.
115–120 (5 min)Close and home taskSlides 21 and 22. Point out the video chapters for home viewing. Restore lab settings, then select Start fresh on this computer at each station.

Teach from the printed run sheet: one page per unit with what to say, the exact clicks to show, the learner task and the review check.

Lab quick card: Windows 10

Click-by-click steps for this week’s tasks in Windows 10 with Microsoft Edge. Windows 11 differences are in brackets. If your screen looks different, ask your instructor.

To do thisSteps
Zoom a webpagePress Ctrl and + to zoom in, Ctrl and - to zoom out, and Ctrl and 0 to reset. Or open the browser menu (··· in Edge, ⋮ in Chrome) and use Zoom.
Make everything biggerStart › Settings › System › Display. Under Scale and layout, choose a size from the Scale menu. (Same path in Windows 11.)
Make only text biggerStart › Settings › Ease of Access › Display › Make text bigger. Drag the slider, then select Apply. (Windows 11: Settings › Accessibility › Text size.)
Choose the sound outputSelect the speaker icon on the taskbar, then the device name above the volume slider, and choose the headset. Or Start › Settings › System › Sound › Choose your output device. (Windows 11: the ˄ arrow beside the slider.)
Mute or unmuteSelect the speaker icon on the taskbar, then the speaker symbol at the left of the volume slider.
Screen brightnessDesktop monitor: use the buttons on the monitor. Laptop: select the battery icon on the taskbar, then drag the brightness slider.
Easier-to-read colorsStart › Settings › Ease of Access › High contrast, turn it on, then choose a theme. (Windows 11: Settings › Accessibility › Contrast themes.)
Check a printer and its queueStart › Settings › Devices › Printers & scanners, select the printer, then Open queue. (Windows 11: Settings › Bluetooth & devices › Printers & scanners.)
Set a default printerIn Printers & scanners, turn off Let Windows manage my default printer, select the printer, then Manage › Set as default.
Print previewPress Ctrl and P. Check Printer, Pages, Copies and Layout before you select Print. Margins are under More settings.
Switch the calendar viewOutlook.com: select Day, Week or Month at the top right of the calendar. Google Calendar: the view menu at the top right (Day, Week, Month).
Set a repeat and a reminderOpen the event. Outlook.com: Repeat › Weekly; Remind me › 30 minutes. Google Calendar: Does not repeat › Weekly; Notification › 30 minutes. Save, then find the next occurrence.
Share free/busy onlyOutlook.com: Calendar › Share › enter the person’s email › Can view when I’m busy. Google Calendar: Settings › Settings for my calendars › your calendar › Share with specific people or groups › See only free/busy (hide details).
Undo an AutoCorrect change in WordType teh and a space; Word changes it to the. Press Ctrl and Z right away to undo. Settings: File › Options › Proofing › AutoCorrect Options.

Using the video

This week the instructor demonstrates every task live. The video repeats each task for home viewing; point learners to its chapters at the close. It pauses on a “Your turn” card after each practice prompt.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Every learner works at their own workstation; a partner coaches but does not take over. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Record one task you want to make easier and one skill you bring.
  • Increase this lesson’s text with browser zoom (Ctrl and +) until it is comfortable. Find and select Next slide. Record the zoom you used, then reset it with Ctrl and 0.
  • Select the headset as the sound output and play the first ten seconds of the Week 1 video (slide 21). Then choose the speakers again. Record where the brightness control is for your screen.
  • Open print preview (Ctrl and P) for page 1 of this worksheet. Record the printer name, orientation and page count. Set Pages to 1 and Copies to 1, then select Cancel. Do not print unless the instructor directs it.
  • Match HDMI, Ethernet and USB to three jobs: an external screen, a wired network and a flash drive. Find one real port on your computer.
  • On paper, marked simulated: write a fictional library appointment with all five fields (title, day, start, end, place), a reminder and a weekly repeat. Reread it and check each field.
  • On slide 14, choose Week view and write what it shows that Day view does not. On slide 15, choose free/busy and write what your partner sees.
  • In Word, type teh and a space; AutoCorrect changes it to the. Press Ctrl and Z to undo it. Then write a four-part help request for a screen that only says folder unavailable: app and version, what you were trying to do, what you tried, the exact message. Record one specific partner suggestion.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
