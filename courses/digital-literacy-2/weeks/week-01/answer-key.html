Week 1 worksheet answer guide

Course home

Instructor guide. Accept equivalent evidence-based answers. These activities evaluate demonstrated skills, not speed.

1. Record one task you want to make easier and one skill you bring.

Accept a personally meaningful goal and existing skill.

2. Increase this lesson’s text with browser zoom (Ctrl and +) until it is comfortable. Find and select Next slide. Record the zoom you used, then reset it with Ctrl and 0.

Browser zoom (Ctrl and +) or the page’s text-size control, a zoom level recorded, and a reset with Ctrl and 0.

3. Select the headset as the sound output and play the first ten seconds of the Week 1 video (slide 21). Then choose the speakers again. Record where the brightness control is for your screen.

Headset selected and tested, then speakers restored. Brightness: monitor buttons on a desktop; Quick Settings on a laptop.

4. Open print preview (Ctrl and P) for page 1 of this worksheet. Record the printer name, orientation and page count. Set Pages to 1 and Copies to 1, then select Cancel. Do not print unless the instructor directs it.

Printer name matches the lab destination; orientation and page count recorded; Pages 1 and Copies 1; nothing printed.

5. Match HDMI, Ethernet and USB to three jobs: an external screen, a wired network and a flash drive. Find one real port on your computer.

External screen: HDMI. Wired network: Ethernet. Flash drive: USB. USB-C features vary by device and cable.

6. On paper, marked simulated: write a fictional library appointment with all five fields (title, day, start, end, place), a reminder and a weekly repeat. Reread it and check each field.

Title, day, start, end, place, a reminder and a repeat; the paper entry is marked simulated.

7. On slide 14, choose Week view and write what it shows that Day view does not. On slide 15, choose free/busy and write what your partner sees.

Week view shows several days side by side to spot conflicts. Free/busy shows the partner only Busy, with no title or place.

8. In Word, type teh and a space; AutoCorrect changes it to the. Press Ctrl and Z to undo it. Then write a four-part help request for a screen that only says folder unavailable: app and version, what you were trying to do, what you tried, the exact message. Record one specific partner suggestion.

AutoCorrect changed teh to the and Ctrl and Z restored it. The help request names the app and version, the task, what was tried and the exact message; the partner suggestion is specific.
