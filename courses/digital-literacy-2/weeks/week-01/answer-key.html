Week 1 worksheet answer guide

Course home

Instructor guide. Accept equivalent evidence-based answers. These activities evaluate demonstrated skills, not speed.

1. Record one task you want to make easier and one skill you bring.

Accept a personally meaningful goal and existing skill.

2. Increase webpage text with browser zoom (Ctrl and +). Record the control you used, then reset it with Ctrl and 0.

Browser zoom (Ctrl and +) or the page’s text-size control; demonstrates reset with Ctrl and 0.

3. Select the headset as the sound output and play a test sound. Record where the brightness control is for your screen, and explain brightness versus contrast.

Headset selected and tested; mute checked. Brightness is light output (monitor buttons on a desktop); contrast is light/dark separation.

4. Match HDMI, Ethernet and USB to three jobs: an external screen, a wired network and a flash drive. Find one real port on your computer.

External screen: HDMI. Wired network: Ethernet. Flash drive: USB. USB-C features vary by device and cable.

5. Create a fictional library appointment: title, day, start and end, place and a reminder. If no calendar app is set up, write the same five fields here on paper, as in video chapter 7, and mark it simulated. Reread it and check each field.

Title, day, start/end, location, reminder; time zone when relevant. A paper entry marked simulated is acceptable when no calendar account is set up.

6. Choose a calendar view and a sharing level for a partner who only needs availability. Explain why.

Week for conflicts; free/busy for limited detail. Verify service-specific options.

7. Open print preview (Ctrl and P) for page 1 of this worksheet. Record the printer name, orientation and pages. Set Pages to 1 and Copies to 1. Do not print unless the instructor directs it.

Printer name matches the lab destination; Pages 1 and Copies 1; orientation recorded; nothing printed without direction.

8. In Word, type teh and a space; autocorrect changes it to the. Press Ctrl and Z to undo it. Record a useful help search and one piece of partner feedback.

Autocorrect changed teh to the and Ctrl and Z restored it; the learner checks suggestions rather than trusting them; the help search names the app and task.
