Make it for a reader

Think about a set of instructions that made a new task easy. Most likely they told you the next step and left nothing to guess. Today you will make a small resource pack for a library computer-help session. You will write a handout in Word, total a supply list in Excel, and build three slides in PowerPoint. Then you will save the handout as a PDF and check it the way your reader will. Every choice starts with the reader. Who will pick up this handout, and what should they do after reading it? Write one sentence that names your reader and their next step. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Who will read your handout? What should they do after reading it? Write it as one sentence. Worksheet: Mission 3A, task 1.

Give the handout real structure

Open a blank Word document and type the handout lines from your worksheet. Select the first line, Computer help at the library. On the Home tab, choose Heading 1. Then make Who it helps and Steps into Heading 2. Bold text only changes how a line looks. A heading style tells Word, and a screen reader, that a new section starts here. To check, open the View tab and turn on the Navigation Pane. Your headings appear in a list on the left. Next, select the three steps and choose Numbering. For the link, select words that name where it goes, such as Read the computer-help guide. Press Control and K, and type the address.

Suggest a change and let the owner decide

A few keys save a lot of mouse work. Control and C copies what you selected. Control and V pastes it. Control and Z undoes your last change, so a mistake is easy to take back. Now trade seats with a partner. In their handout, open the Review tab, turn on Track Changes, and change one word to make a step clearer. Word shows your edit in color instead of quietly replacing the text. A comment is different. It explains why, and it changes nothing. Back at your own seat, choose Accept or Reject for each change. The owner always makes the final call. Build your handout, then trade seats and track one edit. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Type the handout. Apply Heading 1 and Heading 2. Number the steps. Add the link. Trade seats. Track one edit. Accept or reject. Worksheet: Mission 3A, tasks 2 to 4.

Enter the costs, then a formula

Download supplies.csv from your worksheet and open it in Excel. Columns have letters and rows have numbers, so B2 means column B, row 2. The list has paper at 12 dollars, folders at 8, and pens at 5. Change the heading in B1 to Cost in dollars, so a reader knows what the numbers mean. Click cell B5, just below the costs. Type an equals sign, then SUM, then B2 colon B4 in parentheses, and press Enter. The equals sign starts a formula, and the colon means every cell from B2 through B4. B5 shows 25. Before you change anything, predict. If paper costs 15 dollars, what will the total be? Enter the formula in your own sheet and write your prediction. Pause the video now.

Check your prediction and keep the formula

Now test your prediction. Click B2, type 15, and press Enter. The total changes to 28 by itself, because the formula adds the cells, not a number someone typed. If your total stayed at 25, click B5 and read the formula bar. A typed number, or a range that skips a cell, will not update. When it works, save it the right way. Choose File, then Save As, and pick Excel Workbook as the type. A CSV file keeps the numbers but drops the formula. Change paper to 15, check the total against your prediction, and save it as an Excel workbook. Pause the video now.

Three slides, one message

Slides work best when each one does one job. The first slide names the need: Need computer help? The second shows the steps. The third says where to go. Use the same three steps as your handout, so your reader gets one message, not two. Start PowerPoint with a blank presentation and pick one theme on the Design tab, so all three slides match. Keep the words few and large. Then step back from your screen. Can you still read every word? If you have to squint, cut words before you shrink the text.

Crop, describe and credit the photo

Insert the practice photo on the first slide. Cropping trims the edges you do not need, so the two people and the handout fill the frame. Resizing changes the size of the whole picture. They solve different problems. There is a catch. A crop only hides the edges. They stay in the file until you compress the picture and delete the cropped areas. Next, add alt text, one sentence that says what the photo shows, for someone who cannot see it. PowerPoint may suggest a description. Check it the way you would check an AI answer. Then add the credit line. Make your three slides, then crop, describe, and credit the photo. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Make three slides with one theme. Insert the practice photo and crop it. Add alt text and the credit line. Worksheet: Mission 3C, tasks 1 and 2.

Save a PDF, then open it and check

Who gets the file decides the format. A neighbor who only reads it gets a PDF, which looks the same on any computer. A partner who will edit it gets the Word document. A tool that needs plain rows of numbers gets a CSV. Before you export, choose Review, then Check Accessibility. It points out missing headings, missing alt text, and links that do not say where they go. Then choose File, Save As, and set the type to PDF. Saving is not the last step. Open the PDF from your folder and check the headings, the numbered steps, and the link, because that file is what your reader gets. Save your handout as a PDF, open it from the folder, and check it. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Save the handout as a PDF. Open the PDF from Resource pack. Check the headings, steps and link. Worksheet: Mission 3D, task 1.

Trim and split a short clip

Short how-to videos need editing too. Trimming cuts unwanted material at the start or the end. Splitting cuts a clip into pieces, so you can remove a section in the middle, such as a long pause. After every cut, play it back. Did you clip the first word of a sentence? Did you cut the step that explains the result? If the clip has captions, check them against the edited sound, because old captions drift out of time. The video editor in Windows needs a Microsoft account, so today you will use a practice page. Open the Mission 3D practice page, trim the start and end, then remove the pause in the middle. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Open the Mission 3D practice page. Trim the start and end. Remove the pause in the middle. Worksheet: Mission 3D, Round 4.

Check your pack like its reader

Before you call your pack finished, use it the way your reader will. Open the PDF and find the next step without hunting for it. Click the link. In the workbook, change one cost and watch the total follow. Read the second slide from across the room. Each check tests one of today's skills: a handout with real structure, a formula that updates, slides with one message, and an export you opened yourself. Fix the first problem you find, then check again. The same routine works for a club notice, a church bulletin, or a household budget. Make it for a reader, test it, and keep the original.
