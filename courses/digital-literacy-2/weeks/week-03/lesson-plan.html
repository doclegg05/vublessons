Week 3 lesson plan: Create something people can use

Course home

Build a clear resource handout, a small workbook, and a short presentation.

Learning outcomes

  • Structure a document for reading and revision.
  • Use a formula and format a small workbook.
  • Adapt a presentation and media for an audience and credit reused work.

Prepare the room

Provide a workstation and headphones for each learner, this lesson and worksheet, a word processor and spreadsheet app. Use instructor-provided fictional accounts or a modeled demonstration for cloud tasks. Test the local video and printer destination before class. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated. For week 6, supply a plain-text editor and the downloadable starter app; use an approved AI tool only if available.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Pair a driver and coach, then switch. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Identify the audience and action for a fictional computer-help handout.
  • Create a document with a title, two heading styles, a short paragraph, a numbered list and a meaningful link.
  • Use copy, paste and undo; record one place a shortcut helped.
  • Suggest or track one partner edit, then accept or reject it with a reason.
  • Open supplies.csv in a spreadsheet. Add =SUM(B2:B4); verify 25, then change paper to 15 and verify 28.
  • Build three slides: need, steps, help. Use one theme, readable type, useful image alt text and a data table or labeled chart.
  • Crop a copy of the supplied original image. Describe trimming, splitting and captions for a short video. Record any source and license.
  • Export the handout to PDF and reopen it. Record two checks, and explain when editable format or CSV would be better.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
