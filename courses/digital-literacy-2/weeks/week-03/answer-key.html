Week 3 · Answer and observation guide

Assess observable evidence. A spoken prompt is “With prompt”; using the quick card alone can be independent. Mark simulated work separately from hands-on performance. These observations do not change assessment scores.

Mission 3A · Structure, then suggest

  1. Names a specific reader (for example, a neighbor who wants computer help) and one next action (for example, visit the learning desk).
  2. The first line uses Heading 1, and Who it helps and Steps use Heading 2, not bold; the three steps are numbered; both headings appear in the Navigation pane.
  3. The link text names the destination and the link opens; paste and undo worked with no lost text; resource-handout is in the Resource pack folder; one useful shortcut is named.
  4. The edit appeared as a tracked change before the owner selected Accept or Reject; the reason names what the reader needs. Only the owner makes the final choice.

Round 2 · Bold is not a heading

  1. The bold line is missing from the Navigation Pane until Heading 2 is applied; then it is listed. Check Accessibility reports no heading problems, or names a finding the learner fixes, such as missing alt text.

Round 3 · Check the AI draft

  1. The link says click here instead of naming its destination. The hours say 9 p.m., but the library page says 8 p.m. The steps are out of order: choose a task, visit the desk, then ask how to repeat it. The draft ends with no next action. A good rewrite uses Read the computer-help guide as the link text and ends with a step such as Visit the learning desk on a weekday before 8 p.m.

Review: A real heading style carries structure. Bold alone does not tell Word or a screen reader it is a heading.

Mission 3B · Make the total recalculate

  1. B1 reads Cost in dollars; B5 shows =SUM(B2:B4) in the formula bar and 25; a written prediction came before any change.
  2. B5 shows 28 after B2 becomes 15; the file is saved as supplies.xlsx. Saving as CSV would keep the number but drop the formula.

Round 2 · Typed total versus formula

  1. B5 shows 33 because the formula adds the cells. C5 still shows 28 because a typed number never updates. With paper back at 15, B5 shows 28 again.

Round 3 · Add a row

  1. The total is now in B6 and reads =SUM(B2:B5), showing 34 (15 + 8 + 5 + 6). If the range still ended at B4, the learner changed it to B2:B5.

Round 4 · CSV keeps values only

  1. The formula bar shows a plain number, such as 28 or 34, not =SUM. The CSV kept the values and lost the formula, so keep working in supplies.xlsx.

Review: The colon includes the full range and = starts the formula. The comma here leaves out B3.

Mission 3C · Show the message clearly

  1. Three slides in the order need, steps, help; one theme on all three; text readable from the back of the room; the steps match the handout.
  2. The photo is cropped in PowerPoint and the photo file itself is unchanged; the alt text says in one sentence what the photo shows; the credit line names the source and the permission.

Round 2 · Alt text that helps

  1. Best: Two people review a computer-help handout at a library table. Starts with image of: Image of two people. Vague: A photo. Too long: the description of the whole room. The learner's own alt text is one sentence about what matters in the photo.

Round 3 · Crop is not delete

  1. The cropped edges come back when the handles are dragged out, so a crop alone does not remove them. After Compress Pictures with Delete cropped areas of pictures they are gone from the presentation. It matters when something private sits near the edge of a photo.

Round 4 · Use, credit, ask or skip?

  1. Your photo of the sign: use it. VUB practice photo: use with credit. Search result with no license: do not use it. Licensed with credit: use with credit, for example Photo: J. Smith, used under its license. Neighbor's photo: ask first.

Review: Cropping changes the visible frame on the slide. Keep the original photo and check its reuse permission.

Mission 3D · Export, then inspect

  1. The PDF is in Resource pack and opens; the checks name headings, steps and a working link; a reader gets the PDF, an editor the Word document, and a tool that needs rows gets CSV.

Round 2 · Check before you export

  1. The learner names a finding and its fix, or reports none, replaces the PDF and opens the new copy from Resource pack.

Round 3 · Which format for whom?

  1. Neighbor: PDF. Editing partner: DOCX. Treasurer: XLSX, because CSV would lose the formula. Presenter: PPTX. Sign-up tool: CSV. Printing: PDF, so the layout does not shift.

Round 4 · Trim and split a clip

  1. Trimming removes Start (0 to 3 s) and End (17 to 20 s) and leaves 14 seconds. Removing the middle pause (9 to 12 s) leaves 17 seconds. Explain and Next step stay because they carry the speech.

Review: A PDF is a separate file. Reopen that file and inspect what the reader will receive.
