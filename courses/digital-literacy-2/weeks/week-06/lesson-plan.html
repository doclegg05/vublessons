Week 6 lesson plan: Build a small web app with AI

Course home

Turn a clear need into a tested, accessible prototype using basic vibe coding.

Learning outcomes

  • Describe a small app and write a bounded AI prompt.
  • Explain HTML, CSS, JavaScript and browser storage.
  • Test a prototype, revise one behavior and explain what a hosted service still needs.

Prepare the room

Provide a workstation and headphones for each learner, this lesson and worksheet, a word processor and spreadsheet app. Use instructor-provided fictional accounts or a modeled demonstration for cloud tasks. Test the local video and printer destination before class. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated. For week 6, supply a plain-text editor and the downloadable starter app; use an approved AI tool only if available.

Two-hour teaching plan

Minutes from startPhaseInstructor and learner actions
0–10 (10 min)Choose the problemIntroduce a fictional community-resource finder and agree on three observable requirements.
10–30 (20 min)Model a first buildExplain website versus web app versus SaaS. Demonstrate the supplied starter and a bounded AI prompt; no paid account is required.
30–50 (20 min)Build round oneLearners use an approved AI tool or edit the supplied starter. Save a first version before changes.
50–60 (10 min)BreakPause away from screens.
60–75 (15 min)Test demonstrationPlay the explainer; show keyboard, empty-result, narrow-screen and privacy checks.
75–105 (30 min)Improve and retestPairs swap tester/builder roles, fix one observed defect and record evidence.
105–120 (15 min)Demo and reflectionDemonstrate three acceptance checks and complete the extension rubric. Discuss hosting requirements without deploying.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Pair a driver and coach, then switch. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Name the app’s user, problem and three acceptance checks. Keep the scope to one page.
  • Write a prompt naming content, behavior, accessibility and privacy boundaries.
  • Open the starter. Save a copy as resource-finder-v1.html using a plain-text editor. If working offline, no AI tool is required.
  • Find the <h1> heading in the source and replace only its text. In the resources array, change the fictional Library Skills Desk name to Community Skills Desk. Save as resource-finder-v2.html and reopen.
  • Ask an approved AI tool for one small improvement, or make the heading/data edit manually. Record exactly what changed.
  • Test blank input, matching and nonmatching search, mixed case, category filters, keyboard focus and narrow-screen layout. Record expected, actual and pass/fail.
  • Fix one defect or improve one requirement. Repeat its test and one previously passing test. Keep both versions.
  • Demonstrate the app. Explain HTML/CSS/JavaScript, where data lives, and why a public SaaS service needs additional design and maintenance.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
