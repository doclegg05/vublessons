Check which account you are using

Use the account and the tone that fit your role. Check which account you are signed in to before you post or send. Use a different password for each account. A password manager is an app that keeps your passwords safe. Two-step sign-in asks for a second check, such as a code sent to your phone.

To, Cc, and Bcc

To is the person who needs to act. Cc sends a copy that everyone can see. Bcc hides those addresses from the other people. It does not make the message secret, and someone can still send it on. Reply all answers the sender and everyone in To and Cc, so check who needs your answer.

When you send a message matters

Say when you need a reply. Think about how urgent it is and when the reader is at work. Scheduled send lets you write now and pick when the message goes out, such as 9 a.m. tomorrow. It will not fix unclear words, so check the message and the time first.

Work together now, or give feedback later?

Working together at the same time is called live editing. Leaving comments to read later is called feedback later. Pick the way that fits everyone’s schedules and the decision you need.

A good comment says what to change

Try: “The contact method is hard to find. Could we put it after the steps?” Say which text, why, and what you would change.

Answer a comment, then resolve it

Read the comment. Ask if it is not clear. Make the change, or say why you will not, then resolve the comment. A resolved comment is hidden, not deleted. If the file is saved online, such as in OneDrive, version history can bring back an older copy if a change is lost.

Things you buy: products, services and subscriptions

Products are things you can hold. Services are work someone does for you, or access they give you. Streaming plays video or music over the internet while you watch or listen. Netflix, YouTube and Spotify are examples, and hoopla is free with a West Virginia library card. A subscription keeps charging you until you cancel it.

Check a payment before you pay

Know how you pay. A credit card lets you dispute a wrong charge. A digital wallet, such as Apple Pay or Google Pay, pays with a card you saved. Money sent with Zelle, Venmo or Cash App is hard to get back, so pay only people you know. Check the seller, the total and the refund rules first.

Small purchases inside an app add up

A free app may sell extras, and a saved card makes each one a single tap. Turn on a password or approval for purchases, and check your receipts. Deleting an app does not cancel its subscription. Do not buy anything during this practice.
