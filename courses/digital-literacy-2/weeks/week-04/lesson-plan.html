Week 4 lesson plan: Communicate and collaborate with care

Course home

Choose the right channel, give useful feedback, and make safe online choices.

Learning outcomes

  • Channel and account: given two fictional messages (a detailed request a partner can answer tomorrow and an urgent problem in the room), each learner chooses a suitable channel for each and explains why, and names the account a volunteer notice should come from. Both choices fit the urgency and the audience.
  • Email: in the worksheet, each learner writes a practice email with a specific subject, one clear request, a reply-by time and a courteous close; places three fictional recipients correctly in To, Cc and Bcc and says who a Reply all reaches; and rewrites a sentence to spell out abbreviations and remove an assumption about age. All parts are correct with no more than one prompt, and nothing is sent.
  • Feedback: in a practice handout in Word, each learner adds a comment to a partner’s handout that names the place, the change and the reason, then replies to the comment on their own handout, makes or declines the change with a reason, and resolves it. Each learner also chooses live coauthoring or later comments for a given schedule and names the access each role needs.
  • Meetings and communities: using the slide 14 practice meeting, each learner unmutes, raises and lowers a hand and mutes again, tests a headset microphone in Windows Settings when one is available, and lists four meeting or webinar habits, including asking before recording, plus one detail to confirm with an organization before trusting a community post.
  • Purchases and payments: given two fictional streaming offers, each learner works out what each costs for 12 months and for 3 months, writes two questions to ask before paying, and chooses a way to pay a new website with a reason, explaining why person-to-person apps are only for people they know. The arithmetic is correct and the reason matches the payment protections taught.

Prepare the room

Each station: Microsoft Edge, Microsoft Word, a headset (with a microphone if you have them), this lesson and worksheet, and scrap paper or the Windows Calculator for task 8. At the front: the projector showing Edge and Word, and a board for the warm-up answers. Learners have no email, meeting or cloud account, so the email in tasks 2 and 3 is a practice draft in the worksheet, the comments in task 6 happen in a local Word document by swapping seats, and slide 14 stands in for a real meeting; mark this evidence simulated. Show Cc, Bcc, Schedule send, sharing a Word file or version history live only from a demonstration account, never your personal inbox; otherwise use the practice views on slides 4, 5, 6, 11, 13 and 14. No purchase, payment detail or real store page is used. Before class, open a blank Word document and check that Review › New Comment, Reply and Resolve work. Play chapters 2, 4 and 6 with captions to check sound and rehearse the pauses at 1:29 and 2:54. Keep 3–5 printed worksheets in case the network fails. After your own run-through, close Word without saving and select Start fresh on this computer at each station.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated.

Lab quick card: Windows 11

Click-by-click steps for this week’s tasks in Windows 11 with Microsoft Edge. If your screen looks different, ask your instructor.

To do thisSteps
Check which account you are usingIn Edge, select your profile picture at the top of the window. The menu shows the name and email of the account that is signed in. Select Manage profile settings to see more.
Add Cc or Bcc to an emailOutlook.com: in a new message, select Cc or Bcc on the right side of the To line. New Outlook app: Options › Show fields › Show Bcc. Gmail: in a new message, select Cc or Bcc on the To line.
Reply or Reply allReply goes only to the sender. Reply all goes to the sender and everyone on the To and Cc lines, never to Bcc. Check the list before you select Send.
Schedule an emailOutlook.com or new Outlook: select the arrow next to Send, then Schedule send, and pick a time. Gmail: select the arrow next to Send, then Schedule send. Check the time zone.
Add a comment in WordSelect the words, then Review › New Comment (or press Ctrl, Alt and M). Type your comment. If you see a Post button, select it (or press Ctrl and Enter).
Reply to and resolve a comment in WordSelect the comment and type in its Reply box, then post it. When the change is made, select More thread actions (···) › Resolve thread. If the comment shows a Resolve button instead, select it.
Share a Word file to write together (needs OneDrive)Select Share at the top right, above the ribbon. Save the file to OneDrive if asked, enter the person’s email, choose whether they can edit or only view, then select Send. Lab computers have no OneDrive account.
See earlier versions of a Word fileOnly for files saved in OneDrive or SharePoint. Microsoft 365: select the file name at the top of the window, then Version history. Word 2021 and earlier: File › History.
Test your headset microphoneStart › Settings › System › Sound. Under Input, choose the headset, then select the arrow at the right of its name. Select Start test and speak, then Stop test.
Mute and raise your hand in a meetingMicrosoft Teams: Ctrl, Shift and M mutes or unmutes; Ctrl, Shift and K raises or lowers your hand. Zoom: Alt and A mutes or unmutes; Alt and Y raises or lowers your hand.
Turn on meeting captionsMicrosoft Teams: More (···) › Language and speech › Show live captions. Zoom: select Show captions on the toolbar, or More › Show captions. The host can turn captions off.
Cancel a subscriptionMicrosoft: in Edge go to account.microsoft.com/services, sign in, select Manage next to the subscription, then Cancel. Google Play: go to play.google.com/store/account/subscriptions, select Manage, then Cancel subscription. Deleting an app does not cancel it.

Using the chaptered video

Play each chapter inside its cycle, as the Show, from the video slide. Pause at the times named in the plan; the narration does not stop by itself. Ask learners to predict before a demonstration and explain the evidence afterward. Replay only the chapter needed for a refresher.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Every learner works at their own workstation; a partner coaches but does not take over. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Choose a channel for each message and explain the difference: (a) a detailed request that your partner can answer tomorrow; (b) an urgent problem in this room right now.
  • In the box below, write a practice email to a fictional helper, Alex, asking for comments on the computer-help handout. Include To, a specific subject, a greeting, one clear request, when you need a reply, and a courteous close. Nothing is sent.
  • You are sending a notice about a fictional Saturday computer-help session. Alex runs the session, Sam organizes it and should see the notice, and 20 volunteers should not see one another’s addresses. Write who goes in To, Cc and Bcc. If Sam clicks Reply all, who receives it? Choose a time to schedule the notice, and say whether it should come from your personal account or the volunteer group’s account.
  • Rewrite this message so anyone can follow it: “Pls turn on MFA ASAP. Even older folks can manage it.” Spell out each abbreviation, give a clear time, and remove the assumption about age.
  • With your partner, choose who is owner, writer and reviewer for the handout. Say where the one shared copy would live and what access each person needs (viewer, commenter or editor). Would you edit together now (synchronous) or leave comments for later (asynchronous)? Explain why. This is a decision; no file is shared.
  • In Word, type the title “How to get computer help” and two steps: 1. Choose a task. 2. Visit the learning desk. Swap seats: on your partner’s handout, select one step, choose Review, then New Comment, and write a comment that names the place, the change and the reason. Swap back: reply to the comment on your handout, make or decline the change, then resolve it. Record your comment and your decision here. Leave Word open for the final check.
  • Open slide 14. In the practice meeting, unmute, raise your hand, lower it, then mute again. If your headset has a microphone, test it (see the quick card row Test your headset microphone). Then list four meeting or webinar habits, including asking before recording, and one detail you would confirm with the organization itself before trusting a community post.
  • Compare two fictional offers for a streaming TV service. Offer A: $5 a month, cancel any time before it renews. Offer B: $48 a year, paid now, renews every year. What does A cost for 12 months? For 3 months? Write two questions to ask before paying. Which would you use to pay a website you have not used before: a credit card, a debit card, a digital wallet or a person-to-person app such as Zelle? Explain why. Do not open a real store.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
