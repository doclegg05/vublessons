Week 4 lesson plan: Communicate and collaborate with care

Course home

Choose the right channel, give useful feedback, and make safe online choices.

Learning outcomes

  • Choose a communication channel and write a clear email.
  • Collaborate using comments, coauthoring and version history.
  • Explain online purchases, subscriptions and digital identities.

Prepare the room

Provide a workstation and headphones for each learner, this lesson and worksheet, a word processor and spreadsheet app. Use instructor-provided fictional accounts or a modeled demonstration for cloud tasks. Test the local video and printer destination before class. No paid service, real purchase or new learner account is required.

For unavailable features, use the course simulation or role play and label the evidence as simulated. For week 6, supply a plain-text editor and the downloadable starter app; use an approved AI tool only if available.

Two-hour teaching plan

Minutes from startPhaseInstructor and learner actions
0–10 (10 min)ReconnectShare one improvement from the resource-pack review.
10–30 (20 min)Communication modelDemonstrate audience, timing, identity, email fields and channel choice.
30–50 (20 min)Message labPairs draft a fictional request and revise for clarity and inclusive language.
50–60 (10 min)BreakPause away from screens.
60–80 (20 min)Collaboration and commerceDemonstrate coauthoring, comments, meeting controls and purchase review; play the explainer.
80–110 (30 min)Team labComplete the shared-document role play and fictional subscription comparison. Switch roles.
110–120 (10 min)Check and reflectUse knowledge checks and ask each learner to describe one helpful feedback habit.

Facilitation and access

Demonstrate slowly, then let learners try. Offer keyboard and mouse paths, captions and the written transcript. Print large copies if needed. Pair a driver and coach, then switch. Provide extra practice time without requiring learners to disclose a disability.

Evidence to collect

  • Choose a channel for a detailed nonurgent request and for an urgent lab problem. Explain the difference.
  • Draft an email to a fictional library helper: subject, request, context and close. Do not send it.
  • Explain To, Cc and Bcc. Choose an appropriate time and account for your message.
  • Revise one sentence to remove an assumption or unexplained abbreviation.
  • Assign owner, writer and reviewer roles. Choose live coauthoring or later comments and justify access.
  • Write a specific comment on the resource handout. Respond, revise and record the decision.
  • List four meeting or webinar habits, including recording consent.
  • Compare fictional offer A: $5 monthly, cancel before renewal; offer B: $48 yearly, renews annually. Calculate one year of A ($60) and list questions before authorizing either.

Feedback and differentiation

Ask the learner to explain a choice and demonstrate the result. Mark Independent, With prompt, or Needs practice. Re-model only the missing step. For faster learners, ask them to test an edge case or explain an alternative; do not add unrelated tasks.
