Week 4 · Lesson plan

Objectives

  • Write an email request with a subject that names what you need and when. (Gmail (in Microsoft Edge); post-test item 13)
  • Use words that welcome everyone, and offer a choice instead of guessing. (Gmail (in Microsoft Edge); post-test item 15)
  • Put the right people in To and in Bcc, and know what Bcc does and does not do. (Gmail (in Microsoft Edge); post-test item 14)
  • Give feedback on one shared copy: write a specific comment, reply, then resolve it. (Microsoft Word; post-test item 16)
  • Check an offer for when it ends, what it costs, how often you pay, and how to cancel. (Microsoft Edge; post-test item 17)

Observable outcomes

  • Request: in Gmail, each learner writes a subject with the day and time, types the four-line message with a Friday-at-noon deadline and a phone-or-email choice, and changes «guys» to «everyone». Nothing is sent.
  • Recipients: each learner puts desk@example.com in To and the three volunteer addresses in Bcc, does not click Send, closes the draft, finds it in Drafts, and can say what Bcc hides and what it does not stop.
  • Comments: in Word, each learner types the four-line handout, posts the specific comment, adds the Help desk line, replies Added, thank you, resolves the thread, and saves Handout with comments in Documents.
  • Offer: on the practice page, each learner finds when the free days end (day 8), the price ($12), how often it is charged (each month) and how to cancel (Account › Billing › Cancel plan), and writes all four on the worksheet.

How this lesson runs

You project the deck and the class does each step together, one or two clicks per slide. Every step slide names the program in its top corner, gives the clicks, and says what learners should see. The worksheet repeats every step with a check box, so anyone who falls behind can catch up, and adds an If you get stuck box for each part. Wait for every screen to match before moving on.

Each part opens with a slide naming the program, then why it matters, a short video clip, the steps and one check question. Week 4 teaches only the five skills on the post-test (items 13 to 17): a clear request, welcoming words, Bcc, comments on one shared copy and a charge that repeats. The video chapters on choosing a channel, meetings and community posts are not played. There is no break and no timer. Minutes below are a guide for pacing, not a clock for learners.

Quick card

Open Gmail
Click the + for a new tab › type mail.google.com › Enter.
Write a draft
Compose › click Subject › type the message. Never click Send in class.
Use Bcc
At the right end of the To line, click Bcc. Addresses in Bcc are hidden from the other people.
Close and find a draft
Click the X at the top right of the message. Open Drafts on the left.
Add a Word comment
Select the words › Review tab › New Comment › type › Post comment.
Reply and resolve
Click the Reply box, type, click the arrow. Then ··· › Resolve thread.
Save in Documents
File › Save As › Browse › Documents › click in File name, press Ctrl+A, type the name › Save.
Check an offer
Find four facts: when the free days end, the price, how often you pay, and how to cancel.

Week-specific preparation

Each station: Microsoft Edge signed in to a Gmail account (learners use their own from Computer Skills, or a class account), Microsoft Word that opens without a sign-in, and File Explorer. Before class, confirm every learner can sign in to Gmail, that Word can save to Documents on This PC (not only OneDrive), that the practice offer opens from the Week 4 worksheet, and that no old practice drafts are left. All addresses end in example.com, which is reserved for examples and does not accept mail, so a message sent by mistake only bounces back. Check sound on the projector: play the first Watch slide. Keep a roster for the end-of-class check. After your own run-through, discard your practice draft, delete Handout with comments, and select Start fresh on this computer at each station.
