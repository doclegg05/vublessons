Week 4 worksheet answer guide

Instructor guide. Accept equivalent evidence-based answers. These activities evaluate demonstrated skills, not speed.

1. Choose a channel for each message and explain the difference: (a) a detailed request that your partner can answer tomorrow; (b) an urgent problem in this room right now.

(a) A written message, such as email, with the details and a link to the shared handout, so the partner can answer later. (b) Tell the instructor in person now, because it is urgent and here. A phone call or text suits an urgent problem outside the room.

2. In the box below, write a practice email to a fictional helper, Alex, asking for comments on the computer-help handout. Include To, a specific subject, a greeting, one clear request, when you need a reply, and a courteous close. Nothing is sent.

To: Alex. A subject that names the task (for example, Please review the computer-help handout); a greeting; one clear request; a reply-by time (for example, by Thursday at 3 p.m.); a courteous close. A partner reading it can say what to do and by when. The worksheet draft counts as a simulated email.

3. You are sending a notice about a fictional Saturday computer-help session. Alex runs the session, Sam organizes it and should see the notice, and 20 volunteers should not see one another’s addresses. Write who goes in To, Cc and Bcc. If Sam clicks Reply all, who receives it? Choose a time to schedule the notice, and say whether it should come from your personal account or the volunteer group’s account.

To: Alex. Cc: Sam. Bcc: the 20 volunteers, so their addresses stay hidden. Sam’s Reply all goes to you and Alex only; Bcc recipients do not receive a Reply all. Schedule it for working hours, such as 9 a.m. on a weekday. Send it from the volunteer group’s account. Bcc does not encrypt the message or stop forwarding.

4. Rewrite this message so anyone can follow it: “Pls turn on MFA ASAP. Even older folks can manage it.” Spell out each abbreviation, give a clear time, and remove the assumption about age.

For example: “Please turn on multifactor authentication (MFA), a second sign-in check such as a text code, by Friday. I am happy to help if you would like.” Both abbreviations are spelled out or removed, a clear time replaces ASAP, and nothing assumes age or ability.

5. With your partner, choose who is owner, writer and reviewer for the handout. Say where the one shared copy would live and what access each person needs (viewer, commenter or editor). Would you edit together now (synchronous) or leave comments for later (asynchronous)? Explain why. This is a decision; no file is shared.

Owner coordinates and sets access (editor); writer edits (editor); reviewer comments (commenter). One agreed copy, for example in OneDrive or Google Drive. Synchronous when everyone can work at the same time; asynchronous comments when schedules differ. A decision with a reason is enough; real sharing needs an account.

6. In Word, type the title “How to get computer help” and two steps: 1. Choose a task. 2. Visit the learning desk. Swap seats: on your partner’s handout, select one step, choose Review, then New Comment, and write a comment that names the place, the change and the reason. Swap back: reply to the comment on your handout, make or decline the change, then resolve it. Record your comment and your decision here. Leave Word open for the final check.

The comment names a place, a change and a reason (for example, In step 2, add the desk’s opening hours so readers know when to go). The writer replies, makes or declines the change with a reason, and resolves the comment only after that. The comment shows the station’s Word user name, not the reviewer’s.

7. Open slide 14. In the practice meeting, unmute, raise your hand, lower it, then mute again. If your headset has a microphone, test it (see the quick card row Test your headset microphone). Then list four meeting or webinar habits, including asking before recording, and one detail you would confirm with the organization itself before trusting a community post.

The practice meeting is used correctly, and the microphone test shows sound if the headset has a microphone. Habits: test sound first, mute when not speaking, use raise hand, chat or Q&A as the host asks, turn on captions if helpful, ask before recording. In a webinar, attendees usually cannot unmute. Community check: hours, eligibility or cost, confirmed through the organization’s official website or phone number.

8. Compare two fictional offers for a streaming TV service. Offer A: $5 a month, cancel any time before it renews. Offer B: $48 a year, paid now, renews every year. What does A cost for 12 months? For 3 months? Write two questions to ask before paying. Which would you use to pay a website you have not used before: a credit card, a debit card, a digital wallet or a person-to-person app such as Zelle? Explain why. Do not open a real store.

A: $60 for 12 months and $15 for 3 months. B: $48 whether you keep it 3 or 12 months, so B is cheaper only if you keep it 10 months or more. Questions: when does it renew and at what price, how do I cancel, are there refunds, what is included, who is the seller? Payment: a credit card, or a digital wallet that uses one, because a wrong or unauthorized charge can be disputed. A person-to-person app is for people you know; money sent is hard to get back. No real purchase or payment details.
