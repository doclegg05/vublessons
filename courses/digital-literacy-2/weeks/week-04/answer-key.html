Instructor guide. Accept equivalent evidence-based answers. These activities evaluate demonstrated skills, not speed.

1. Choose a channel for a detailed nonurgent request and for an urgent lab problem. Explain the difference.

Email for detailed nonurgent context; direct instructor conversation for urgent local issue.

2. Draft an email to a fictional library helper: subject, request, context and close. Do not send it.

Specific subject, clear request, sufficient context, correct recipient and courteous close.

3. Explain To, Cc and Bcc. Choose an appropriate time and account for your message.

To primary; Cc visible others; Bcc concealed recipient list, not encryption. Work hours/urgency and right identity.

4. Revise one sentence to remove an assumption or unexplained abbreviation.

Respectful specific language with no age/culture/ability assumptions.

5. Assign owner, writer and reviewer roles. Choose live coauthoring or later comments and justify access.

Roles and permission fit; synchronous together or asynchronous across time.

6. Write a specific comment on the resource handout. Respond, revise and record the decision.

Specific change plus reason; reviewer decision recorded.

7. List four meeting or webinar habits, including recording consent.

Audio test, mute/raise hand, useful chat, consent before recording.

8. Compare fictional offer A: $5 monthly, cancel before renewal; offer B: $48 yearly, renews annually. Calculate one year of A ($60) and list questions before authorizing either.

$60 versus $48 nominal year; also compare needs, features, refunds, renewal and cancellation. No real transaction.
