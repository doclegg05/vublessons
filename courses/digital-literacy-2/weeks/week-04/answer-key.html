Week 4 · Answer and observation guide

Assess observable evidence. A spoken prompt is “With prompt”; using the quick card alone can be independent. Mark simulated work separately from hands-on performance. These observations do not change assessment scores.

Mission 4A · Send a clear request

  1. (a) A written message, such as email, with the details and a link to the shared handout, so the partner can answer later. (b) Tell the instructor in person now, because it is urgent and here. A phone call or text suits an urgent problem outside the room.
  2. To: Alex. A subject that names the task (for example, Please review the computer-help handout); a greeting; one clear request; a reply-by time (for example, by Thursday at 3 p.m.); a courteous close. A partner reading it can say what to do and by when. The worksheet draft counts as a simulated email.
  3. To: Alex. Cc: Sam. Bcc: the 20 volunteers, so their addresses stay hidden. Sam’s Reply all goes to you and Alex only; Bcc recipients do not receive a Reply all. Schedule it for working hours, such as 9 a.m. on a weekday. Send it from the volunteer group’s account. Bcc does not encrypt the message or stop forwarding.
  4. For example: “Please turn on multifactor authentication (MFA), a second sign-in check such as a text code, by Friday. I am happy to help if you would like.” Both abbreviations are spelled out or removed, a clear time replaces ASAP, and nothing assumes age or ability.

Review: Reply all addresses the sender and visible To/Cc recipients. Always inspect the actual recipient list.

Mission 4B · Improve one shared copy

  1. Owner coordinates and sets access (editor); writer edits (editor); reviewer comments (commenter). One agreed copy, for example in OneDrive or Google Drive. Synchronous when everyone can work at the same time; asynchronous comments when schedules differ. A decision with a reason is enough; real sharing needs an account.
  2. The comment names a place, a change and a reason (for example, In step 2, add the desk’s opening hours so readers know when to go). The writer replies, makes or declines the change with a reason, and resolves the comment only after that. The comment shows the station’s Word user name, not the reviewer’s.

Review: Specific feedback identifies where and what to improve. Explain why it helps the reader.

Mission 4C · Join with care

  1. The practice meeting is used correctly, and the microphone test shows sound if the headset has a microphone. Habits: test sound first, mute when not speaking, use raise hand, chat or Q&A as the host asks, turn on captions if helpful, ask before recording. In a webinar, attendees usually cannot unmute. Community check: hours, eligibility or cost, confirmed through the organization’s official website or phone number.

Review: Ask before recording or sharing. Respect the group’s rules and each person’s boundaries.

Mission 4D · Check before paying

  1. A: $60 for 12 months and $15 for 3 months. B: $48 whether you keep it 3 or 12 months, so B is cheaper only if you keep it 10 months or more. Questions: when does it renew and at what price, how do I cancel, are there refunds, what is included, who is the seller? Payment: a credit card, or a digital wallet that uses one, because a wrong or unauthorized charge can be disputed. A person-to-person app is for people you know; money sent is hard to get back. No real purchase or payment details.

Review: Deleting an app usually does not cancel billing. Follow the provider’s cancellation process and retain confirmation.
