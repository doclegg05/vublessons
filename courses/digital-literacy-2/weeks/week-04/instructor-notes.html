Week 4 · Instructor notes

Slide 1

Work together without confusion.

Welcome learners by name. Tonight the class works together: you show each step on the projector and everyone does it at their own computer. F toggles fullscreen; N shows notes.

Slide 2

Start with what you know.

Two or three answers. Listen for clear titles, numbered steps and pictures that help: tonight teaches exactly those.

Slide 3

Tonight's goals

Read each goal aloud. Each part of tonight teaches one goal, in one program. Learners check the goals off on their worksheet at the end of class.

Slide 4

How tonight works.

Each part starts with a slide that names the program. Wait for every screen to match before you move on.

Slide 5

Open the course website

If the lab browser opens to another page, the address bar is still the long box at the top. If stuck: No Edge on the taskbar? Press the Windows key, type Edge and press Enter.

Slide 6

Open your Week 4 worksheet

Learners may also have a printed copy. The worksheet tab stays open for the practice offer link in Part 4. If stuck: Cannot find Week 4? Roll the mouse wheel toward you to scroll down. The weeks are in order.

Slide 7

Now working in: Gmail (in Microsoft Edge)

Part 1 of 4. Say the program name out loud. Everyone opens Gmail in a new Edge tab. Anyone who cannot sign in is the first thing to help.

Slide 8

A request someone can act on.

Ask: which message in your inbox was easy to answer? Usually it said what and by when. Tonight we write one, and fix a word that leaves people out.

Slide 9

Watch: a message someone can act on

Plays 1:25 to 2:10 of the Week 4 video and stops on its own. It ends before the part about checking recipients, which is Part 2. Press Play; it never starts by itself. Captions are on.

Slide 10

Open Gmail

Check that every screen reaches an inbox before going on. Help anyone who cannot sign in now. If stuck: A sign-in page? Sign in with your Gmail address and password, the way you do in Computer Skills. Ask your instructor if you cannot.

Slide 11

Start a new message

Tonight is drafts only. Say it now: nobody clicks Send. If stuck: No Compose button? Make the Edge window full size: double-click the very top edge of the window.

Slide 12

Find your way around the message

Point to each part on the projector. Say it twice: tonight we only write drafts, and nobody clicks Send. Every address ends in example.com, which cannot receive mail.

Slide 13

Type the subject

The subject carries the request and the deadline: Friday noon. If stuck: Typed it in the wrong box? Click in the Subject box, press Ctrl and A, and type the subject again.

Slide 14

Type the message

The first line says «Hi guys,» on purpose. We fix it in the next step. Read the lines aloud slowly. If stuck: Made a typing mistake? Press Backspace to erase the letter before the blinking line, then type it again.

Slide 15

Make the words welcome everyone

A word like guys leaves some people out. Everyone leaves nobody out. The phone line is the other fix: it offers a choice. If stuck: The comma disappeared? Click right after everyone and type , again.

Slide 16

Read it like your reader

Could the reader act without asking what you meant? Walk the room and read a few subjects aloud.

Slide 17

Which subject line is easiest to act on?

Ask everyone to choose before you select an answer. Correct answer: Reply by Thu 5 p.m.: bring the sign-in sheet?. It says what you need and when, so the reader can act.

Slide 18

Which invitation is the most welcoming?

Ask everyone to choose before you select an answer. Correct answer: Bring a guest if you like.. It does not guess who someone is or who they live with. It offers a choice.

Slide 19

Still working in: Gmail (in Microsoft Edge)

Part 2 of 4. Say the program name out loud. We stay in Gmail, in the same message. Do not close it.

Slide 20

To, Cc and Bcc.

Ask: when is it a problem if 30 people can see each other's addresses? Bcc protects addresses. It does not make the message secret.

Slide 21

Watch: who receives it

Plays 2:12 to 2:32 and stops on its own, before the part about Reply all, which we are not practicing tonight. Press Play; it never starts by itself. Captions are on.

Slide 22

Add who answers

This is the organizer's own desk address. The volunteers go in Bcc. If stuck: Gmail shows a list of names under the box? Keep typing the whole address and press Enter. Ignore the list.

Slide 23

Open the Bcc line

Bcc stays out of the way until you ask for it. If stuck: Cannot find Bcc? Look at the right end of the To line. Cc and Bcc are two small words. Click in the To line first.

Slide 24

Add the volunteers

These three people do not know each other. Bcc keeps their addresses from the others. If stuck: The bubbles did not appear? Press Enter or Tab right after you type each address.

Slide 25

Check who sees what

Say it: the three in Bcc cannot see each other. They can still forward the message.

Slide 26

Close the draft

Closing with the X is safe. Gmail saves drafts by itself. If stuck: Asked whether to save or discard? Choose to save it. Gmail keeps a draft automatically.

Slide 27

Find your draft

Leave the draft in place. Your instructor checks it at the end of class. If stuck: No Drafts folder in the list? Click More in the list on the left, then Drafts.

Slide 28

You email 12 members of a community group who do not know each other, and you put their addresses in Bcc. What does Bcc do?

Ask everyone to choose before you select an answer. Correct answer: Each person cannot see the other addresses. Bcc hides addresses from the other recipients. It does not stop forwarding or protect the message.

Slide 29

Now working in: Microsoft Word

Part 3 of 4. Say the program name out loud. Everyone opens Word in a moment. Tonight one person plays both roles: reviewer, then owner.

Slide 30

One copy. Comments, not copies.

Ask: has anyone received three versions of the same file? In real life the shared copy lives in one place everyone can open. Tonight we practice the comment steps on one file.

Slide 31

Watch: why one copy

Plays 2:59 to 3:15 and stops on its own, before the roles and the live-or-later discussion. Press Play; it never starts by itself. Captions are on.

Slide 32

Watch: a useful comment

Plays 3:48 to 4:21 and stops on its own, before the partner pause prompt. Press Play; it never starts by itself. Captions are on.

Slide 33

Open Word

Say the keys aloud: Windows key, type Word, Enter. Wait until every screen shows Word. If stuck: Word did not open? Click Start, scroll the list of apps to Word, and click it.

Slide 34

Start a blank document

Walk the room. Everyone should see an empty blank document. If stuck: Already looking at an empty page? Your computer skips the start screen. Go on to the next step. A sign-in box? Close it with its X; no account is needed.

Slide 35

Find your way around the Review tab

Point to each part on the projector. Tonight one person plays both roles on one copy. In real life the copy lives in one shared place, and each person comments on that same file.

Slide 36

Type the handout

Spelling does not need to be perfect. The comment is the point. If stuck: Made a typing mistake? Press Backspace to erase the letter before the blinking line, then type it again.

Slide 37

Be the reviewer: select a line

You practiced dragging in Week 3. Demonstrate it once more, slowly. If stuck: Lost the shading? Click anywhere on the page and try the drag again, slowly.

Slide 38

Add a comment

The comment is tied to the words you selected. If stuck: No New Comment button? Click the Review tab first. It is in the row of tabs above the buttons.

Slide 39

Write a specific comment

Point out where, what and why: after the steps, add the contact number, so readers can find help. If stuck: No Post comment button? Press Ctrl and Enter together.

Slide 40

Be the owner: make the change

Use the number on the slide. A comment is a request; the owner decides and makes the change. If stuck: The new line is shaded or crossed out? Click anywhere else on the page. The shading was the selection.

Slide 41

Reply to the comment

The reply tells the reviewer the change is done. If stuck: No arrow button? Press Enter, or Ctrl and Enter together.

Slide 42

Resolve the comment

Resolved comments are hidden from view, not deleted. They stay in the file. If stuck: A Resolve button instead of three dots? Click Resolve. Your Word shows it that way.

Slide 43

Save: open the Save As box

The same three clicks as in Week 3. If stuck: Not sure you clicked File? File is the first word in the row of tabs, above the buttons.

Slide 44

Choose Documents

Documents is the folder learners used last week.

Slide 45

Name it and save

Check a few screens: the window title should read Handout with comments. If stuck: Typed the name next to the old one? Click in the box again, press Ctrl and A, and type the name again.

Slide 46

You added the number the comment asked for. What next?

Ask everyone to choose before you select an answer. Correct answer: Reply "Added", then resolve the comment. The reply tells the reviewer. Resolving closes the comment.

Slide 47

Now working in: Microsoft Edge

Part 4 of 4. Say the program name out loud. We are back in Edge, on a practice page. Everyone starts from the worksheet tab.

Slide 48

A free trial can become a monthly charge.

Ask: has a free trial ever turned into a charge? The four facts are the habit. The offer tonight is fictional.

Slide 49

Watch: review an offer

Plays 6:01 to 6:27 and stops on its own, before the video discusses logos and the practice wrap-up. Press Play; it never starts by itself. Captions are on.

Slide 50

Open the practice offer

The offer opens in a new tab, so the worksheet stays open beside it. If stuck: Cannot find the worksheet tab? Click the tabs at the top of Edge. The one named Week 4 · Worksheet has the link.

Slide 51

Find when the free days end

Day 8 is the first day a charge happens. That is the end of the free trial. If stuck: Cannot find the small print? Roll the mouse wheel toward you to scroll down. The small print is at the bottom.

Slide 52

Find the price and how often

This is the recurring charge: the same amount again and again.

Slide 53

Find how to cancel

A real offer that hides this line is a warning sign. If stuck: No cancel line on your screen? Scroll down. It is the third line of the small print.

Slide 54

Write the four facts

Walk the room. Accept short notes such as: day 8, $12, every month, Account Billing Cancel plan.

Slide 55

Free for 7 days, then $12 each month. You do nothing. What happens?

Ask everyone to choose before you select an answer. Correct answer: $12 is charged every month until you cancel. A monthly price keeps charging until you cancel.

Slide 56

Show your instructor.

Walk the room with the roster. Mark Independent, With prompt or Needs practice for each of the five. Re-show only the missing step. Check the draft before anyone deletes it.

Slide 57

Delete your practice draft

Do this after your instructor has checked the draft. A discarded draft leaves nothing in the learner's account. If stuck: No trash can on the draft? Click the three dots at the bottom right, then Discard draft.

Slide 58

I can…

Celebrate one concrete result per learner. Anything unchecked is the first thing to practice next week.

Slide 59

Take it home.

Learners delete the practice draft, then close all programs. Select Start fresh on this computer. Handout with comments may stay in Documents.

Slide 60

Mission complete.

Collect evidence, not just confidence. Restore shared settings.
