Plan a help session people can follow

Think of a group task you have helped organize, such as a family gathering or a volunteer day. Confusion usually starts when people assume someone else knows what to do. Today we plan a fictional Saturday computer-help session. You wrote the handout. Sam will review it. Pat organizes the session and keeps the shared copy. Alex runs the session on the day, and twenty volunteers need to hear about it. You will send a clear request, improve one shared copy, take part in a meeting, and check an offer before you pay. The goal is not more messages. It is the right person taking the right next step.

Choose the channel and the account

Start by choosing how to reach someone. A detailed request that can wait until tomorrow suits an email, because the details stay in writing. An urgent problem in this room needs a word in person, right now. A shared document suits work that several people change. Then check which account you are sending from. In a new email, the From line shows it. A personal account and a volunteer group account reach different people and carry different expectations. Choose a channel for each of the two messages on your worksheet, and say why. Pause the video now.

Pause card. A detailed request for tomorrow: which channel? An urgent problem in this room: which channel? Say why for each. Worksheet: Mission 4A, task 1.

Write a request someone can act on

A useful email answers three questions. What should I do, with which file, and by when? A subject such as Please review the computer-help handout by Thursday tells Sam the job before the email is even opened. In the body, greet Sam, name the part to check, say when you need a reply, and close politely. Spell out abbreviations, and drop guesses about anyone's age or skill. Then read it as Sam would. Could Sam act without asking you a question? Write the request to Sam, then rewrite the unclear message on your worksheet so anyone could follow it. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Write the request to Sam: subject, request, reply-by time. Rewrite the unclear message for any reader. Read it as Sam would. Worksheet: Mission 4A, tasks 2 and 4.

Check who receives it

Before you send, read every recipient line. Sam needs to act, so Sam goes in To. Pat should know, so Pat goes in Cc. The volunteers do not need a review request, so take them off. The notice to the volunteers is a different email. There, Alex goes in To, Pat in Cc, and the twenty volunteers in Bcc, so they cannot see one another's addresses. Bcc does not stop anyone from forwarding it. If Pat chooses Reply all, it goes to you and Alex, never to the Bcc names. Use Schedule send so an early draft arrives at a sensible hour. Address the volunteer notice on your worksheet. Pause the video now.

Work from one shared copy

Imagine three people editing three copies of the handout and calling each one final. Nobody knows which instructions to follow. Instead, agree on one shared copy and one place to keep it. Pat, the owner, makes the final call. You write. Sam reviews. Give each person only the access the job needs. Sam can comment, and you and Pat can edit. Working at the same time is called synchronous. Leaving comments for later is called asynchronous. Neither is better. Pick the one that fits people's schedules, and write the decision in the shared copy. With a partner, choose your roles, your shared place, and how you will work. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Choose owner, writer and reviewer. Pick one shared place and each person's access. Together now, or comments for later? Worksheet: Mission 4B, task 1.

Give feedback the writer can use

Compare two comments. This is confusing gives the writer nothing to act on. In step 2, add where the desk is, Room A on the first floor, so first-time visitors can find it. That comment names a place, a change, and a reason. In Word, select the step, choose Review, then New Comment, and type it. The writer replies first, then makes the change or explains why not. Resolve the comment only after that reply. A resolved comment is hidden, not deleted. Comment on the work, never on the person. Trade seats, add one useful comment, then reply to and resolve the comment on your own handout. Pause the video now.

Join a meeting ready to take part

Before a meeting, test your headset. In Windows Settings, under Sound, choose the headset as the input and speak during the test. In the meeting, stay muted unless you are speaking, and raise your hand when the host asks for it. Turn on captions if they help. A webinar is different. The host controls who can speak, so questions go in the Q and A box or the chat. Ask before you record anyone, especially a group sharing personal stories. If your sound fails partway through, use the backup the host gave you, such as the chat or the shared notes.

Check a community post before you trust it

A community page can point you to a pantry, a class, or an event. A member's post is a lead, not a fact. It might be old, about a different place, or simply wrong. Read the group's rules, and keep private details out of public questions. For hours, eligibility, or what to bring, check the organization's own page or call its listed number. That is the same habit you used with AI answers in Week 2. Open the Mission 4C practice page and practice the meeting controls. Then list your meeting habits and one community claim you would check yourself. Pause the video now.

Check an offer before you pay

Here are two fictional streaming offers. Offer A is free for 30 days, then 6 dollars a month until you cancel. Offer B is 60 dollars a year, paid now. Free does not mean free forever. After the first month, A charges you every month, so a full year of A costs 66 dollars. Before you agree, find the seller, the price now, the price later, the renewal date, and how to cancel. Deleting the app does not cancel the subscription. Pay with a credit card, which lets you dispute a wrong charge. Person-to-person apps are for people you know. Work out both offers for 3 months and for 12, and write two questions to ask. Pause the video now.

Pause card. Work out Offer A and Offer B for 3 months. Then for 12 months. Write two questions to ask first. Worksheet: Mission 4D, task 1.

Leave a clear record of the next step

Good coordination leaves a trail anyone can follow. By the end of today, Sam knows what to review and by when. Pat can see the notice without the volunteers seeing one another. Everyone works from one shared copy, and every comment ends with a reply and a decision. In meetings, you test first, take turns, and ask before recording. Before you pay for anything, you know what it will cost in a year and how to stop it. Pick one habit to use this week: a clearer subject line, fewer recipients, one shared copy, or a check before you pay.
