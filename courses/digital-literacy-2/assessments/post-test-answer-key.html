Assessment version 2

One point per correct answer. Domain totals: four points each. Total: 28. Accept only the keyed choice for the knowledge score; use worksheet rubrics for demonstrated skills.

1. You are reading a community workshop page at home. Its words are too small, but your email app and desktop labels are readable. What should you try first?

C. Enlarge the webpage with browser zoom.

Browser zoom changes the size of webpage content. Display scaling affects the wider desktop, and brightness changes light output. Start with the control that matches the problem, then check the result.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.1 · week 1

2. A community group is choosing a meeting time. The organizer needs your available times, not the details of your personal appointments. Which calendar information should you share?

B. Free/busy times without appointment details.

Free/busy sharing answers the scheduling question without exposing appointment details. Check the actual sharing settings and what the other person can see; sharing a whole calendar may reveal more than the task needs.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.2 · week 1

3. You begin typing a volunteer’s email address, and autocomplete selects another person with a similar name. The message is meant for only one volunteer. What should you do before sending?

A. Check the full address and select the intended person.

Autocorrect and autocomplete offer suggestions, not guarantees. Check names, full addresses and meaning yourself. A correctly spelled word or familiar contact can still be wrong for this message.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.3 · week 1

4. You need 15 copies of a library workshop flyer. You have changed the page layout since its last printout. Which step best avoids wasting paper?

C. Check the destination and preview, then inspect one printed copy.

The selected printer may be left over from another task, and a layout change can alter the output. Confirm the destination and preview, then inspect one test copy before sending the remaining copies.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.5 · week 1

5. You are ready to answer a community group question in your volunteer role. Your browser has opened your personal profile instead. What is the best next step?

B. Select the volunteer profile and confirm who will see the reply.

The active account determines the identity attached to a post. A signature or profile picture does not switch accounts. Confirm both your role and the intended audience before posting.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.1 · week 4

6. On Sunday evening, you finish a handout for a team that reviews work on weekdays. Feedback is needed by Thursday. Which message plan gives a clear, reasonable expectation?

A. Send during the team’s usual hours and ask for feedback by Thursday.

Match message timing to urgency and the group’s expectations. A specific deadline tells the recipient when to act. For a nonurgent task, an urgent label or repeated reminders creates needless pressure.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.2 · week 4

7. A new volunteer asks you to explain a term in a shared handout. They have not used this program before. Which response respects their experience and supports learning?

C. Define the term and connect it to the task they are trying to do.

Explain the unfamiliar term and connect it to the task. One question does not tell you someone’s overall ability. Plain language and an offer of help support learning without making assumptions.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.2 · week 4

8. You need to change a reminder in an updated calendar app. A saved tutorial shows a menu that is no longer there. How should you look for instructions that fit?

B. Check current official help for the app and the reminder task.

Current official help is a good starting point when menus change. Identify the app version and the task, rather than relying only on old button positions. When asking for help, describe what you tried and what happened.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.3 · week 1

9. You want an in-person computer class at a public library near Elkins, West Virginia. Which search is the best starting point for this task?

A. Elkins WV public library computer classes

Useful search terms name the service, place and setting you need. These searches are starting points, not proof that a class exists. Confirm current details with the library before making plans.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.1 · week 2

10. An online directory says a nearby community center offers free computer repairs. It does not say where that information came from. What should you do before relying on the offer?

C. Confirm the service through the center’s official contact information.

Verify a service with the organization responsible for providing it. Search position, appearance and repeated claims do not establish current availability. Check the details that matter for your visit.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.2 · week 2

11. You need a local workshop you can attend in person. A reliable community-center page lists a class in another state with no online option. What is missing for your task?

B. A class relevant to the place where you can attend.

Accuracy and relevance are different checks. A trustworthy page can describe a real class that does not fit your location or attendance needs. Use the source that answers your actual question.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.2 · week 2

12. A fictional community-center form lets you request a workshop place. Some fields are optional and do not seem related to booking. What is the best approach before completing the form?

A. Check the collector and purpose, then provide only information needed for the task.

Check who collects the information, the purpose and the required fields. Optional does not mean necessary. In class, use fictional details; review the completed request and read its confirmation.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.3 · week 2

13. Your community workshop guide has three main sections. You want clear structure that assistive technology can recognize, not just titles that look different. What should you use?

C. Built-in heading styles at the appropriate level.

Heading styles give section titles structural meaning, which helps navigation and assistive technology. Bold, font size and spacing can change appearance without creating that structure.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.1 · week 3

14. Your practice supply list has 6 notebooks in C2, 4 folders in C3 and 3 pens in C4. You want the total quantity to update if any entry changes. Which formula should you use?

B. =SUM(C2:C4)

The colon includes every cell in the range, including the middle row. Adding only the first and last cells misses an item. A fixed number can look correct now but will not update when an input changes. Test the formula by changing one input.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.1 · week 3

15. You want to adapt a diagram from a website for a library workshop handout. You have not checked its license or permission terms. What should you do first?

A. Check whether adapting it is allowed and what attribution is required.

Find the actual license or permission and follow its conditions, including whether changes are allowed and what credit is needed. Credit, a screenshot or free distribution does not by itself establish permission. Use an approved alternative if permission is unclear.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.3 · week 3

16. A coauthor must correct the wording in your community resource guide before it is finished. Which sharing choice lets them make those changes directly?

C. Share the editable file with suitable editing access.

Direct revision needs both an editable format and permission to edit. A screenshot shows the content but does not provide editable paragraphs. A view-only link also prevents the intended task; a checked PDF can be useful later for finished distribution.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.4 · week 3

17. You are emailing a community organizer to ask when the resource workshop starts. Which subject line most clearly describes your request?

B. Please confirm the resource workshop start time

A useful subject names the topic and the action needed. A vague subject hides the request, while saying something is confirmed incorrectly suggests the question is already settled. Put the needed context in the message as well.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.1 · week 4

18. You are emailing a library event reminder to several volunteers. They should receive the message without seeing the full list of other recipients’ addresses. Which field fits?

A. Bcc for the addresses that should be hidden.

Bcc hides those recipient addresses from other recipients; To and Cc normally show them. Bcc does not encrypt the message, stop forwarding or make an attachment safe.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.1 · week 4

19. A fictional video service offers a free month, and you only want to try one lesson. What information do you need before accepting the offer?

C. Renewal timing, the later price and cancellation terms.

Look beyond the introductory price to renewal and cancellation terms. Removing an app is not the same as following the service’s cancellation process. The class uses fictional offers and does not require a real purchase.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.2 · week 4

20. A fictional free app offers an optional tool for a one-time $3 charge. You are reviewing the offer in class. What should happen before anyone authorizes that upgrade?

B. Review the separate charge and purchase terms before deciding.

The download price and the price of an extra feature are separate. Read the purchase details before authorizing anything; do not assume when or whether a charge applies. No real payment is needed for the practice.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.2 · week 4

21. A community team meets online and edits one shared workshop outline together during the meeting. What type of collaboration is this?

A. Synchronous work because people are collaborating at the same time.

Synchronous means working together at the same time. Asynchronous work happens at different times, such as leaving comments for tomorrow. Online storage and separate computers do not determine which timing pattern the team uses.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.1 · week 4

22. You want a volunteer to point out unclear steps in a draft. They should add feedback without directly changing the document. The tool supports comments. Which permission should you give?

C. Commenter access to that volunteer.

Commenter access supports feedback without direct rewriting. Viewer access does not provide the requested commenting ability in this scenario, and a public editing link gives more access than the task requires.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.1 · week 4

23. A workshop guide uses an abbreviation without explaining it. New readers may not know what it means. Which comment would best help the author revise the guide?

B. Spell out the abbreviation the first time so new readers can follow the steps.

Useful feedback identifies the specific problem, suggests a change and explains how readers benefit. Vague criticism leaves the author guessing. A cosmetic change does not supply missing information.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.1 · week 4

24. You would like to save a video discussion from a library workshop for later study. The recording would include other participants. What should guide your next step?

A. Follow the organizer’s recording rules and confirm the needed permission.

The presence of a Record button or an invitation does not establish recording permission. Check the organizer’s rules and the appropriate permissions before recording participants. A helpful purpose does not replace that check.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.2 · week 4

25. A volunteer cannot comfortably use a mouse, and another studies with the sound off. Which workshop page would let both people complete the task?

C. One with keyboard access and accurate captions for spoken content.

Flexible input and captions address different needs. Providing both lets more people complete the same task in different situations. Supporting one need does not remove the other barrier.

Domain: Safety and Security · objective 7.1 · week 5

26. In a fictional community forum, someone you have never met builds a friendly relationship and asks you to keep a request for private information secret. Their identity is unverified. What should you do?

B. Withhold the information and check their identity independently.

A false identity used to build trust is called catfishing. This scenario does not prove the person is an impostor, but friendliness and local photos do not verify identity. Pause and check through a separate trusted route before sharing private information.

Domain: Safety and Security · objective 7.2 · week 5

27. An unfamiliar person leaves a USB drive labeled “useful workshop files” at the community computer lab. Staff have not approved it. What should you do?

A. Keep it disconnected and ask authorized staff what to do.

An unknown drive can carry harmful software. Its label and appearance do not prove it is safe, and switching computers does not remove the risk. Let authorized staff handle it without connecting it yourself.

Domain: Safety and Security · objective 7.3 · week 5

28. You are protecting a stored practice document. Preventing edits is not enough; someone without the key should be unable to read its contents. Which protection matches that need?

C. Encrypting the stored data.

Encryption protects readable data by requiring the appropriate key or authorized access. Read-only settings restrict changes, renaming changes a label, and an ordinary ZIP is not automatically encrypted. An unlocked account or device still needs protection.
