Assessment version 3

One point per correct answer. Domain totals: four points each. Total: 28. Accept only the keyed choice for the knowledge score; use worksheet rubrics for demonstrated skills.

1. At a community computer lab, text is too small in the word processor, email app and desktop menus. Enlarging one webpage did not help those other areas. Which setting best matches the wider problem?

C. Increase the computer’s display scaling, then check the apps.

Display scaling affects the wider desktop and its apps. Browser zoom changes webpage content, so it does not solve small menus and text across other programs. Make one adjustment, then check the result.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.1 · week 1

2. You are arranging two volunteer visits on different days next week. Both are saved in your calendar. Which view would best help you compare their times with your other appointments?

B. Week view, which shows the days together for checking conflicts.

Week view helps compare appointments across several days. Day view provides detail for one day; month view gives a broader overview. Check the saved start and end times when looking for conflicts.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.2 · week 1

3. You create a repeating reminder for a Monday library visit. When you check next week, the reminder appears on Tuesday. What should you do before relying on it?

A. Correct the repeat settings and check the next occurrence.

An automated rule repeats its settings, even when those settings are wrong for the task. Inspect the schedule, correct it and verify the next occurrence instead of assuming automation guarantees the intended result.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.3 · week 1

4. Your print preview for a community flyer shows the last line on a second page. You need each flyer to fit on one sheet. What should you do before making copies?

C. Adjust the layout, check the preview again and inspect one test print.

Preview reveals output problems before you spend paper. Correct the layout without dropping needed content, then inspect a test print. Copy count does not fix a page break.

Domain: Technology Basics · objective 1.5 · week 1

5. You are setting up a separate account for volunteer work. You already have a personal account with a strong password. Which plan best keeps the two accounts protected?

B. Use a different strong password and enable multifactor authentication where available.

The lesson recommends separate credentials, a trusted password manager and multifactor authentication where available. A reused password can expose more than one account if compromised. Shared passwords also weaken individual control.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.1 · week 4

6. A teammate drafts this nonurgent message: “Look at the flyer soon.” You need their review before the community team meets on Friday. Which revision sets a clear response expectation?

A. “Please check the flyer’s contact details and send corrections by Thursday afternoon.”

A useful request states what needs checking and when a response is needed. A clear deadline supports planning; vague timing, an unnecessary urgent label and repeated messages do not clarify the task.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.2 · week 4

7. A volunteer guide begins, “Older people will need someone else to do this for them.” The class includes people with varied experience. Which replacement best supports everyone’s participation?

C. “Try these steps at your own pace, and ask for help where you need it.”

Inclusive language avoids assuming ability from age. Offer clear steps, time to practice and help based on the person’s needs. Learners bring different prior experience and should have a chance to participate.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.2 · week 4

8. A library practice document will not save. You have tried Save As, but the app still displays “Folder unavailable.” Which request would give official support the most useful starting information?

B. Name the app and version, the save task, what you tried and the exact error.

A precise help request names the application, task, attempted steps and error. This helps someone give instructions that fit. A password is not needed to explain the problem.

Domain: Digital Citizenship · objective 2.3 · week 1

9. Searching for “classes” returns cooking, exercise and computer lessons across the country. You need beginner computer instruction near Charleston, West Virginia. Which revision would make the results more useful?

A. Search for “beginner computer classes Charleston WV.”

Refine a broad search with the subject, audience and location. These terms describe the task more clearly; search results still need checking for current availability and suitability.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.1 · week 2

10. A page selling a paid computer course says it is the only reliable way to learn. It provides no evidence for that claim. How should you judge the recommendation?

C. Consider the seller’s interest and compare the claim with independent evidence.

A source’s purpose and interests can shape its claims. Check evidence and compare relevant sources. A commercial interest does not make every statement false, but it is a reason to examine the support for a recommendation.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.2 · week 2

11. You find an official local workshop announcement from three years ago. It accurately describes the event held then, but you need a session you can attend now. What still needs checking?

B. Whether the program still runs and what its current schedule is.

Currency matters even when a source is accurate and local. Verify whether the service still operates and whether dates, eligibility or arrangements have changed before relying on an older announcement.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.2 · week 2

12. After submitting the fictional computer-help form, you see a confirmation for “phone help,” although you intended an in-person visit. What does checking this confirmation tell you to do?

A. Use the available correction or contact method to fix the requested help type.

The confirmation is evidence of what the system received. Check that it matches your intended request, then use the stated correction or contact process. Repeated submissions can create duplicates rather than repair the original.

Domain: Information Management · objective 3.3 · week 2

13. Your handout’s section titles look large and bold, but the document’s heading navigation does not list them. What is the most useful correction?

C. Apply real heading styles to the section titles.

Visual formatting alone may leave titles as ordinary paragraphs. Heading styles add document structure that navigation and assistive technology can recognize. Check the structure as well as how the page looks.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.1 · week 3

14. A practice budget shows three costs: 12, 8 and 5, with a total of 25. You want to check whether the total really uses all three cells. Which test gives useful evidence?

B. Change 12 to 15 and check that the total updates to 28.

Changing an input checks calculation behavior. The total should increase by the same amount, from 25 to 28. If it does not, inspect the formula’s range and whether inputs are stored as numbers; formatting is not a calculation test.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.1 · week 3

15. A practice image has written permission allowing reuse and cropping if the creator, title, source and license are credited. You want to crop it for a flyer. Which plan follows those stated conditions?

A. Crop a copy and include the required credit with the reused image.

Read and follow the permission actually supplied. In this fictional example, cropping is allowed but the stated credit is still required. Save a copy so the original stays available.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.3 · week 3

16. Your finished community handout is ready for neighbors to read and print. They do not need to edit it, and you want to preserve its page layout. Which delivery fits that purpose?

C. Export a PDF, then open it to check page breaks, links and missing content.

PDF is useful for distributing a finished layout. Open the exported file to inspect it; export success alone does not prove the result is usable. CSV is for simple table data and does not preserve this document’s layout.

Domain: Content Creation · objective 4.4 · week 3

17. Your email to a community organizer already has a clear subject. The body only says, “Here is the flyer.” You need the organizer to check the room number before printing. What should you add?

B. A specific request to verify the room number, with the deadline and attached draft.

The body needs enough context and a clear next action, even when the subject is good. State what the recipient should check and when, then verify the intended recipient and attachment before sending.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.1 · week 4

18. You sent a volunteer announcement using Bcc. A partner says this also prevents recipients from forwarding its contents. Which statement correctly explains the limit?

A. Bcc hides those recipient addresses; it does not stop forwarding the message.

Bcc controls visibility of recipient addresses, not what recipients can do with the content. It does not encrypt the message or prevent forwarding. Consider the content’s sensitivity before sending it.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.1 · week 4

19. A fictional app trial says: “No charge for seven days; then $8 each month unless canceled.” You only need the app for one weekend. Which conclusion follows from these terms?

C. Keeping the trial active after seven days can start a recurring charge.

Read the later price and renewal schedule, not just the introductory offer. Follow the stated cancellation process if you do not want renewal. Removing a shortcut does not establish cancellation. This is a fictional offer, not a real purchase.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.2 · week 4

20. A fictional free game offers a $2 upgrade. Its purchase screen also offers a way to require approval for future purchases. Why is that control useful?

B. It can help prevent additional purchases from being authorized without review.

Purchase controls help manage later transactions; they do not erase charges or turn paid features into free ones. Review the offer, authorization settings and receipts. Class practice does not require making a real purchase.

Domain: Communication · objective 5.2 · week 4

21. Two volunteers cannot work at the same time. One can draft a handout today, and the other can review it tomorrow. Which plan fits their schedules while keeping one shared copy?

A. Leave comments in the shared draft for the other person to review later.

Asynchronous collaboration supports work at different times. A shared draft and clear comments let the next person continue the task. A live session does not fit these schedules, and unmanaged copies can create conflicting versions.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.1 · week 4

22. A named reviewer has finished commenting on your draft. You now ask that person to make the agreed corrections directly, while everyone else should still only read it. What access change fits?

C. Give that reviewer editing access and keep the others as viewers.

Match access to the current task and the named people who need it. Direct corrections require editing access; that does not justify giving every reader the same permission. Review sharing after the work is complete.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.1 · week 4

23. A partner comments, “Add the help desk’s contact method after step 3 so readers know where to ask questions.” You are responsible for revising the handout. What is a useful response?

B. Add the verified contact information and acknowledge how you addressed the comment.

Constructive collaboration includes acting on specific feedback and communicating what changed. Resolve a comment when its issue is addressed, or ask a focused question if the requested change is unclear. An unrelated visual change does not solve the stated problem.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.1 · week 4

24. During a live workshop call, background noise from your room makes it hard for others to hear the speaker. You are listening and do not need to speak yet. What should you do?

A. Mute your microphone and unmute when it is your turn to speak.

Meeting etiquette includes controlling background noise, taking turns and using mute appropriately. Muting your microphone stops your room’s sound from being sent; speaker volume and recording controls do not solve that problem.

Domain: Collaboration · objective 6.2 · week 4

25. A practice page marks completed steps only in green and unfinished steps only in red. Some learners cannot distinguish those colors. Which change makes the status clearer?

C. Add visible “Complete” and “Not yet” labels beside the colored indicators.

Do not rely on color alone to communicate meaning. Text labels provide the same information for people who perceive colors differently and for situations where color is hard to see.

Domain: Safety and Security · objective 7.1 · week 5

26. Notifications from a community group keep pulling you away from your practice task. You feel you must check every alert so you do not miss out. Which response supports your own learning goal?

B. Adjust nonessential alerts and choose a planned time to check the group.

Fear of missing out can encourage repeated checking. Choose notification settings, breaks and checking times that support your own goals. You can remain part of a group without responding to every alert immediately.

Domain: Safety and Security · objective 7.2 · week 5

27. A webpage that only displays a written community schedule asks to use your camera. Nothing in your task needs a photo or video call. What is the most appropriate choice?

A. Deny camera access because the permission does not fit the task.

A permission request should fit a clear task and a trusted app. Reading a written schedule does not need the camera. A logo is not a reason to grant unrelated access; review the setting if a legitimate need arises later.

Domain: Safety and Security · objective 7.3 · week 5

28. Your practice laptop uses device encryption, but you leave it unlocked and signed in at a shared table. What protection is still needed when you step away?

C. Lock the session so another person cannot use the open account.

Encryption can protect stored data without the required key, but it does not prevent someone using an already unlocked session. Lock or sign out of the device as appropriate when leaving it. File names do not control access.
